# Vault Annex — diamond-sutra conventions

The methodology guidelines (`0-VAULT-Structure.md`, `../../1-SOURCES/About Sources.md`, `../../2-RAILS/About Rails.md`, `../../3-TRANSFORMATIONS/About Transformations.md`) are **text-agnostic** — they apply to any Railroads vault built on any classical text. This annex records the conventions that are specific to *this* vault: **the Diamond Sūtra (Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra)**.

When the Guidelines and this annex disagree on a vault-specific detail, this annex wins — **for the points it actually states**. Everything it does not state follows the defaults unchanged.

> **Status (2026-10-04):** sections 1, 2, 3, 3a and 7 were filled by the `aligned-corpus-intake` skill when the corpus was ingested (2026-10-02), and updated on 2026-10-04 when the Sanskrit, the Tibetan root and the Tibetan commentaries were rebuilt from the new Dzongsar Markdown exports and Wikisource (Route B, standing decisions of `heart-sutra-rails`; report: `0-INBOX/dorjeechoepa-intake-report.md`). They state facts of the ingest — a human contributor should review them. The Chinese files are still those of 2026-10-02 (see the ID-migration log). Sections 4–6 have nothing to record yet.

## 1. The text

This vault serves **the Diamond Sūtra** — *Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra* (Tib. *'phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa rdo rje gcod pa*, Chin. 金剛般若波羅蜜經), a Mahāyāna Perfection of Wisdom sūtra, read here through its Tibetan Kangyur translation with its Indian, Tibetan and Chinese commentaries.

The root is the Sanskrit text (the source language); the Tibetan is its translation, and the Tibetan commentaries point at the Tibetan, which is the text they were written on (standing decision D1 of `heart-sutra-rails`). The Chinese commentaries comment on Kumārajīva's Chinese translation (and one on Bodhiruci's), so the Chinese translation is in turn the root of those commentaries.

## 2. Addressing scheme

### Sanskrit root and Tibetan — ids from the Wikisource TOC (since 2026-10-04)

**`verse_id_format`:** `section-paragraph` · **Format example:** `^8-12`, heading `^8-0`

Both files carry the thirteen headings of the Derge Kangyur text's Wikisource Index page (`2-RAILS/Sections/Raw/toc-wikisource/vajracchedika.md`; Tibetan verbatim, Sanskrit editorial renderings). Headings are `^<n>-0`; body blocks `^<section>-<n>`; the title, Indic title and homage before section 1 are `^0-<n>`. One block per row of the Dzongsar docs: the Tibetan display doc (430 rows; rows 38 and 317 split at headings 5 and 10, as on the Wikisource page) and the Sanskrit side of the Sanskrit–Tibetan doc (434 rows). Each Tibetan block transcludes the Sanskrit block(s) its row is paired with (`pair.own_side`, the Tibetan side of that doc, is the display cut with six empty rows and one row split, carried onto the display by letters; 102 pairings corrected by Claude, for the text expert to check).

### Chinese files of the 2026-10-02 intake — flat `^N` (unchanged)

The Tibetan root has no chapter structure of its own in the source. Its block `^N` is **segment N of the numbered root the Dzongsar team aligned the commentaries against** (431 typed numbers, `It is for reference only/…Root text.docx`), so a commentary's alignment number *is* the root block id: `1-3` transcludes `^1 ^2 ^3`. The 430-row Tsadel split of the same text (segments 14+15 and 183+184 joined, two later letter corrections) is recorded in the root's sidecar. Translations aligned row-for-row keep the root's ids where their row matches one segment (`lzh-kumarajiva-tibetan-order.md`, `sa-vajracchedika.md`).

`lzh-kumarajiva.md` uses the same flat form for its own numbering: block `^N` is **segment N of the 127 numbered segments** of the Dzongsar Chinese text (MFF4994FD) — the numbers every Chinese commentary refers to. `zh-baihua.md` takes the id of the first Kumārajīva segment it renders. `lzh-bodhiruci.md` keeps the row numbers of its source doc (with gaps).

### Commentaries — headings by outline path, body by top-level section

| Markdown | Role | Anchor |
| -------- | ---- | ------ |
| `#` | Title of the work | `^0` |
| `##` | Top-level division (*sa bcad* 1, 2, 3 … / 分 / 卷 / heading) | `^N-0` |
| `###` … `######` | Deeper *sa bcad* levels | full path + `-0`, e.g. `^3-4-2-6-7-7-0` |

Body blocks are `^<top-level>-<n>`, counted through deeper headings (`verse_id_format: section-paragraph`); blocks before the first heading are section `0`. In the Tibetan commentaries the outline numbers are the human TOC numbers in the source docs; in the Chinese commentaries, headings are numbered in order of appearance. A number written in front of a commentary segment is the id of the root segment(s) it comments on and is transcluded as written; unnumbered segments have no alignment.

### ⚑ Registered deviations — overrides of the default conventions

### ⚑ Flat `^N` for the Chinese translations of the 2026-10-02 intake — overrides `annotation-conventions.md` §1a (registered 2026-10-02; narrowed 2026-10-04)

The default is `^chapter-verse` with a chapter 0. The 2026-10-02 Tibetan root carried no chapters, and its human segmentation was a flat sequence of 431 numbered segments. Since 2026-10-04 this applies only to the Chinese files still in `1-SOURCES/Translations/` from that intake (`lzh-kumarajiva`, `lzh-kumarajiva-tibetan-order`, `lzh-bodhiruci`, `zh-baihua`); the Sanskrit and Tibetan follow their TOC.

### ⚑ Section-paragraph ids from an outside TOC for the root and its translation (registered 2026-10-04)

The root has no outline of its own; its TOC is the Wikisource Index page's (D4), carried onto the Sanskrit through the row pairing with editorial Sanskrit labels (D5). A heading that stands inside a row on the proofread page splits the row there (D7: Tibetan rows 38 and 317); the row's Sanskrit segment is shown with part 2. Standing decisions D1–D13 of `heart-sutra-rails` (2026-10-03).

### ⚑ Commentary rows and alignment made by Claude (registered 2026-10-04)

The three Tibetan commentaries exist in this intake only on Wikisource; no human segmentation or alignment of them was in the raw data. On the vault owner's instruction (2026-10-04) Claude cut them into rows (at every Wikisource heading, before each passage where the commentary takes up a new root segment, and at sentence boundaries in long stretches) and aligned each row to the Tibetan root segments it comments on (`0-INBOX/temp/ws_comm_align.py`, then a reading review; evidence in `0-INBOX/temp/align-<id>/`). Their frontmatter says `alignment_status: needs-review`. Rule 3 of `aligned-corpus-intake` (transclusions only from a human alignment) is set aside for these three files by that instruction.

## 3a. Typed folder — `1-SOURCES/Annotations/`

Declared here as the one additional typed folder under `1-SOURCES/` (`About Sources.md` §2). It holds one `<stem>.annotations.json` per source file, written by `aligned-corpus-intake`. It is the lossless record of the human annotation that markdown cannot carry: per-block provenance (raw doc, paragraph, row), typed alignment prefixes, colour/bold/italic layers with their meaning, Word reviewer comments, variant readings, secondary alignments, and the reference-numbering concordances. It is part of the ground truth, regenerated only with its source file.
