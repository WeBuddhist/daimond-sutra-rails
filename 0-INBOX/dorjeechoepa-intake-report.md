## How the commentaries were segmented and aligned (Claude)

No human segmentation or alignment of these three Wikisource texts exists in the raw data, so both were made by Claude (`0-INBOX/temp/ws_comm_align.py`; evidence in `0-INBOX/temp/align-<id>/`):

1. **Text**: every proofread page, page markup and pecha line breaks removed, letters verbatim; the title line kept as row 1; Wikisource headings become headings.
2. **Rows**: cut at every Wikisource heading; at the sentence boundary before each passage where the commentary takes up a new root segment; and at sentence boundaries inside stretches over 2,000 characters.
3. **Matching**: the commentary's quotations of the sūtra found as syllable sequences shared with the display root, kept in order by a longest monotonic chain (4-syllable sequences, then 3-syllable sequences inside each gap). A segment counts as quoted at ≥ 5 consecutive syllables (shorter segments: all but one), or woven pieces covering ≥ 40 %. An unquoted segment goes with the row of the quoted segment before it (a row comments from its lemma to the next lemma).
4. **Reading review**: eight reviewers read every row against the root and corrected placements — Kamalaśīla 108, Chone 67, Vasubandhu 74 corrections, plus 4 by Claude after the independent review (Kamalaśīla root `^12-1`–`^12-3` moved to the row that opens with their lemma; Vasubandhu's colophon no longer transcludes the sūtra's closing title), each with its Tibetan evidence (`align-<id>/overrides.yaml`). Most moved segments off summary/outline sections and recaps onto the row of the actual lemma, or moved dialogue formulas (རབ་འབྱོར་འདི་ཇི་སྙམ་དུ་སེམས།) to the row that takes up their question.
5. **Generated pair**: `wikisource-<id>/<id>(root-comm).md` (commentary rows) and `<id>-root(root-comm).md` (row N = the display segments it comments on, verbatim); the build carries the latter onto the display by letters (`min_overlap: 12`).

**Quality check against the Dzongsar team's own alignment of these commentaries** (the 2026-10-02 intake, other edition, in git history; read-only comparison, `0-INBOX/temp/eval_alignment.py`, nothing copied): of the root segments both place, same row — Chone **391/408 (96 %)**, Kamalaśīla **252/284 (89 %)**, Vasubandhu **202/229 (88 %)**; most of the rest one row away. Kamalaśīla and Vasubandhu now place more segments than the humans did (420 and 415 vs 286 and 230) because a row is taken to cover the root from its lemma up to the next one.
