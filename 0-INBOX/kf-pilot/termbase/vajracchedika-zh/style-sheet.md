# Style sheet: Chinese (Traditional characters) termbase of the Tibetan Diamond Sutra

Kumārajīva is abbreviated K below; Gemini means the zero-shot modern Chinese line in `terms.json`.

## Policies

1. Script: Traditional characters only, in standard modern forms (恆河, 燃燈佛, 著), with no simplified or Japanese variants; this is the target script of both registers.
2. Punctuation: full-width 。，、；：？！ with 「」 and 『』 for nested quotes, 《》 for titles, （） for brackets, no spaces inside Chinese text; Chinese punctuation matches the register file and the K aligned text.
3. No Latin letters or diacritics appear in a rendering (no Sanskrit in brackets, no Arabic digits); numerals inside terms are written in Chinese characters (三十二相, 七寶, 四句偈, 十億), so each term has one written form.
4. Academic weighting: Kumārajīva's attested Diamond Sūtra wording comes first, then established modern Chinese Buddhist usage, and Gemini is only a check; K is the register's stated preference and the aligned text proves it.
5. Where K is silent or shorter than the Tibetan (many examples have no `kumarajiva` line), take the established Chinese Buddhist term (善逝, 現等覺, 正等覺者, 具壽); never invent a calque and never copy a Gemini gloss unless it is itself an established term.
6. Gemini is not evidence of usage: it mixes Xuanzang-style forms (波羅蜜多, 般涅槃, 具壽) with long explanatory glosses (法門, 一合相) and is inconsistent (長老 and 具壽 both for āyuṣmat). Where it disagrees with K on a term K attests, K wins; where Gemini's form is also an established term and K has no equivalent, it may be adopted.
7. Where K's wording blurs a distinction the Tibetan makes, keep the Tibetan distinction with an established term: 想 (saṃjñā) versus 相 (lakṣaṇa, nimitta); 具壽 (āyuṣmat) versus 長老 (sthavira); 福德 versus 福德聚 (skandha); this is what the register file demands, so K's 我相 becomes 我想 and 法相 becomes 法想.
8. K's transliterated stage and title terms are kept in the academic register (阿耨多羅三藐三菩提, 阿羅漢, 須陀洹, 斯陀含, 阿那含, 般若, 波羅蜜, 涅槃, 菩薩, 須彌山) rather than Xuanzang's 預流, 一來, 不還, 波羅蜜多; one tradition keeps the whole glossary consistent. K's 滅度 is not used for nirvāṇa; parinirvāṇa is 般涅槃 and "nirvāṇa without remainder" is 無餘涅槃.
9. ཆོས has three senses and each gets its own word: teaching is 教法 (bare 法 never means the teaching), phenomena is 法 or 諸法 (K's 一切法, 非法, 善法), and qualities of a Buddha is 佛法 (never used for the teaching); this keeps the English split intact in a language where 法 covers all three.
10. Fixed compounds that contain 法 with the teaching sense are retained as established words (說法, 法門, 正法, 法身, 法眼, 法衣, 法性) and are not rewritten to 教法; the glossary lists them as exceptions so agents do not "correct" them.
11. Plurals and totality: 諸 is used only where the Tibetan has the plural marker རྣམས (諸法), 一切 only for ཐམས་ཅད; Chinese has no number, so these particles must track the Tibetan mechanically.
12. Epithets of the Buddha are one fixed word per Tibetan term and are used identically in vocative, narration and series: 世尊 (bhagavat), 如來 (tathāgata), 善逝 (sugata), 阿羅漢 (arhat, also inside the epithet series), 正等覺者 (samyaksaṃbuddha), 佛 (buddha); in a series they keep the Tibetan order joined by 、 (如來、阿羅漢、正等覺者); K's habit of writing 佛 for bhagavat in narration is not followed.
13. Titles and honorifics: āyuṣmat is 具壽 placed before the name (具壽須菩提), a bare name is used when the Tibetan has none (K's 須菩提！); bhikṣu and its feminine are 比丘 and 比丘尼, lay followers 優婆塞 and 優婆夷, as in K; sthavira is 長老.
14. Names: personal and place names follow K's established characters, adjusted for the Tibetan referent: 須菩提, 舍衛城 (register form, not K's 舍衛國), 祇樹給孤獨園 as one unit, 燃燈佛, 釋迦牟尼, 須彌山, 恆河, 轉輪聖王; where K names a different referent from the Tibetan (K's 歌利王 for the king of Kaliṅga) follow the Tibetan's Sanskrit and say so in the note.
15. The title is written 《能斷金剛般若波羅蜜經》 (parts: 能斷金剛 + 般若波羅蜜 + 經), not K's short 《金剛般若波羅蜜經》, because the Tibetan title includes the cutter (vajracchedikā) and the English glossary says Diamond Cutter; the long Sanskrit-style title of the colophon is 《聖能斷金剛般若波羅蜜大乘經》, built from the same parts plus 聖 (ārya) and 大乘 (mahāyāna).
16. The cosmological measure is not computed or split: སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་སྟོང་ཆེན་པོའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས is always 三千大千世界 in the academic register (K and Gemini agree) and 十億個世界 for children (1000 cubed); a lone lokadhātu is 世界 and loka is 世間 so the three never merge.
17. The "A is not A, therefore called A" formula keeps the same rendering of the term in all three slots, in K's frame (所謂X者，即非X，是名X) for the academic register and 「所謂X，其實不是X，所以才叫X」 for children; this keeps terms recognisable inside the sutra's repeated pattern.
18. Children's register: names that are everyday Chinese stay (佛陀, 世尊, 如來, 菩薩, 眾生, 須菩提, 舍衛城, 釋迦牟尼); transliterated technical terms are replaced by a short plain phrase that translates the same concept, taking the approved English children's rendering as the sense anchor; the register file forbids transliterated technical Sanskrit, and a replacement is never an explanation or a different idea.
19. Children's phrases are at most about eight characters, contain no brackets or explanatory clauses, and are used identically every time (one rendering per sense); a repeated technical idea (e.g. 菩薩 or 福氣) must never vary for style.
20. Compounds take the renderings of their parts in Tibetan order unless the compound is on the lexicalised list (菩薩摩訶薩, 阿耨多羅三藐三菩提, 三千大千世界, 般若波羅蜜, 七寶, 四句偈, 三十二相, 無餘涅槃); patterns: X-saṃjñā is X想 (children's 有「X」的想法), X-grāha is 執著於X (抓著「X」不放), X-dṛṣṭi is X見 (有「X」的看法), X-phala is X果, so a new compound can be assembled from the core table.
21. Variant Tibetan spellings (e.g. བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔ, ཚེ་དང༌ལྡན་པ, མདོ) and duplicate lemmas take exactly the rendering of the base term, so the same Tibetan word is never rendered two ways because of orthography.
22. Mantras and dhāraṇī are not translated: academic gives the Chinese sound transliteration with the usual mantra characters (南無, 唵, 莎訶) and the entry is flagged for review because no K text aligns; children's uses 咒語 with no transliteration.
23. Style of rendering: academic is modern written Chinese (白話書面語), with classical particles used only inside fixed K terms; children's is plain spoken-style Chinese in short sentences, and neither register adds doctrinal explanation to a term.
