## Policies

1. Academic uses "awakening" (not "enlightenment") for བྱང་ཆུབ and its compounds, and "awakened" for -སངས་རྒྱས་པ; Gemini's majority, and it matches the Tibetan "wake/bloom" sense and the Buddha-as-"awakened" etymology (DharmaMitra's "enlightenment" is not used). Children's also uses "awakening"/"awake".
2. Academic uses "affliction" (not "defilement") for ཉོན་མོངས་པ; Gemini attests it, it is the standard equivalent of kleśa, and it keeps "defilement" free for any later stain-word. Children's uses "troubling feeling".
3. ཆོས is one Tibetan lemma with two sense entries, never left as a bare "dharma": (a) teaching = academic "Dharma" (capital), children's "teaching"; (b) phenomena = academic "phenomenon", children's "thing"; the sense is chosen per occurrence and compounds inherit it. This avoids the DharmaMitra/Gemini drift between "Dharma", "dharma" and "phenomena" and the collision of two senses under one word.
4. The third use of ཆོས inside སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ཆོས is rendered "qualities of a Buddha" in academic (DharmaMitra attests it); to keep it distinct, ཡོན་ཏན is rendered "good quality".
5. སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ is "great being" in academic and "bodhisattva great being" for the full phrase; Gemini attests it, it is the calque of the Tibetan, and mahāsattva is not a word academic English normally keeps outside the fixed phrase. Children's: "great hero" and "great awakening hero".
6. སྐྱེས་བུ་ཆེན་པོ is "great man" (academic) and "great person" (children's), never "great being", so it does not collide with policy 5 (Gemini attests "great man").
7. Academic keeps Sanskrit untranslated only for the words academic English normally keeps: Tathāgata, Sugata, arhat, bodhisattva, Buddha, Mahāyāna, nirvāṇa, sūtra, Dharmakāya, and Sanskrit proper names and titles; everything else is translated (so "perfection", not pāramitā; "Blessed One", not Bhagavān; "form body" for གཟུགས་ཀྱི་སྐུ and གཟུགས་སྐུ, not Rūpakāya). This follows the academic register definition and what both translations agree on.
8. Children's has no Sanskrit except names; names lose their diacritics (Subhuti, Shravasti, Dipankara) and "Buddha" stays; Sanskrit epithets become plain phrases (Thus-Gone One, Well-Gone One, worthy one). The mantra and dhāraṇī passages are not rendered syllable by syllable in children's but referred to as "a sacred chant".
9. Academic uses full IAST diacritics on every retained Sanskrit word and name (ā ī ū ṛ ṃ ṅ ñ ṇ ś ṣ, e.g. Dīpaṃkara, Śrāvastī, Anāthapiṇḍada, Gaṅgā, Kaliṅga); both translations already use IAST, and the academic register requires it. Children's has no diacritics.
10. Capitalisation: the head form is lower case except for names, titles and epithets (Subhūti, Blessed One, Tathāgata, Sugata, Teacher), "Buddha"/"Buddhas" (always capital, including inside compounds such as Buddha-field and Buddha eye), "Dharma" in its teaching sense (Dharma discourse, Dharma eye, Dharma robe), and text titles (Diamond Cutter); the honorific before a name stays lower case ("the venerable Subhūti"). The Perfection of Wisdom is capitalised only as the name of the text or class of texts; the practice is "perfection of wisdom".
11. Place names (academic): Śrāvastī, Jetavana (the register's own form, over "Jeta Grove"), Mount Sumeru, Kaliṅga, and the river as "Gaṅgā river" (not Ganges); Gemini attests Gaṅgā and the register wants the standard Sanskrit form. Children's keeps the established English "Ganges River" (DharmaMitra attests it), "Jeta Grove", "Mount Sumeru", "Kalinga".
12. The cosmological measure སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་སྟོང་ཆེན་པོའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས is "three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system" in academic, built from its parts (Gemini's main form, DharmaMitra's "three-thousandfold great thousand world system" is the same), not the coinage "trichiliocosm", which both translations use only as an occasional gloss; children's is "universe of a billion worlds" (new; thousand times thousand times thousand; check the number with a specialist before locking).
13. འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས is "world system" (two words, no hyphen; both attest it), འཇིག་རྟེན is "world", ཁམས is "realm"; children's uses "world realm" (Gemini attests it) for འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས.
14. Compounds take the locked renderings of their parts in the same order and pattern ("X of Y", noun adjuncts only where attested, e.g. Dharma eye, Buddha eye); where the two translations disagree on a compound, take the one that matches the parts (e.g. ཆོས་ཉིད is "nature of phenomena", Gemini, not DharmaMitra's "nature of reality"; དཔེ་བྱད is "minor mark" to pair with "mark").
15. The four bases of the false-grasping formula are fixed as self (བདག), sentient being (སེམས་ཅན), life-force (སྲོག), person (གང་ཟག), and carry through every compound: grasping at / view of / perception of a self, a sentient being, a life-force, a person. Academic "life-force" (DharmaMitra) beats "living soul", which imports a soul idea the Tibetan lacks; children's: self, living being, life, person with "clinging to / view of / idea of".
16. འདུ་ཤེས is "perception" in academic everywhere (both translations attest it; Gemini's "notion" is dropped so compounds stay uniform); children's "idea".
17. འཛིན་པ is "grasping" in academic (both attest it; Gemini's "apprehension" is dropped and kept free for དམིགས་པ); children's "clinging".
18. ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ is "perfection", and "perfection of X" for the named ones (perfection of wisdom, perfection of patience), "six perfections" for ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག; both attest it, and pāramitā is not kept. Children's: "perfection" bare, "perfect wisdom", "perfect patience".
19. སེམས་བསྐྱེད, སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པ and སེམས་པ་བསྐྱེད་པ all share one rendering, "generate the mind" (DharmaMitra; Gemini's "give rise to"/"produce" is dropped to keep the verb uniform); children's "set the mind".
20. One rendering per Tibetan lemma, and no English rendering is reused for two lemmas: ཡོངས་སུ་གྲུབ་པ is "perfect accomplishment" (Gemini), not "perfection"; ཡེ་ཤེས is "pristine wisdom" (new) so ཤེས་རབ stays "wisdom"; མཚན is "mark" and མཚན་མ is "sign".
21. Variant spellings of the same Tibetan share one rendering: བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ and བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔ; གང་གཱའི་ཀླུང and གངྒཱའི་ཀླུང; ཚེ་དང་ལྡན་པ and ཚེ་དང༌ལྡན་པ; མདོ and མདོ་སྡེ ("sūtra"); འཕགས་པ in all its uses.
22. Head forms are singular and lower case, hyphenated where both translations hyphenate (life-force, stream-enterer, once-returner, non-returner, Buddha-field, non-virtuous, four-line verse, wheel-turning monarch); the translator pluralises and capitalises in context.
23. The fruits and stages are "stream-enterer", "once-returner", "non-returner", "arhat", and "fruit of a stream-enterer" etc. (both attest all of them); children's keeps the same English compounds, with "worthy one" for the arhat.
24. Tibetan titles are translated (Diamond Cutter; the Perfection of Wisdom that Cuts Like a Diamond), while the Sanskrit title and mantras in the text are given in IAST and not translated in academic; this follows what both translations do with the colophon title and the mantras.
25. Where a term has attested renderings that differ only in number, case or the article, the lower-case singular is the head form, and the choice between attested renderings follows the higher combined count unless a policy above says otherwise.
