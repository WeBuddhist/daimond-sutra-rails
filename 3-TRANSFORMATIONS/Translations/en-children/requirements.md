# Requirements — Diamond Sutra, English, children's version

## Audience and register
Children aged 8–12, reading on their own or being read to (see `audience.md`). Warm, clear, simple English that a ten-year-old understands on first hearing.

## Source of meaning
- Each segment is retold from the academic translation of the same segment, checked against the Tibetan. Keep the meaning of every segment: who speaks, what is asked, what is answered, what is compared, what is denied.
- Do not add ideas, morals, explanations or examples that the segment does not contain. Do not drop a point the segment makes.
- Keep one children's segment per source segment, with the same id, in the same order.

## Vocabulary
- Use the locked children's rendering from `termbase.md` for every listed term, every time.
- No Sanskrit except names; names without diacritics (Subhuti, Shravasti). "Buddha" stays "Buddha".
- Never make a deep idea false to make it simple: emptiness is not "nothing"; "no self" is not "you don't exist".

## Style
- Short sentences (mostly under 15 words). Everyday words. Active voice.
- The sutra repeats itself on purpose; keep the repetition, but say it simply and the same way each time.
- Paradoxes stay paradoxes ("what is called X is not X; that is why it is called X") — phrase them plainly, don't resolve them.
- Speech in quotation marks. Headings: short and plain.
