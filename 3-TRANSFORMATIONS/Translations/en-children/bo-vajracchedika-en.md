## Setting the mind on awakening ^4-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-1]]

Subhuti, someone who has correctly entered the awakening hero's vehicle should think like this. 'I must help all kinds of living beings. This includes those born from eggs, and those born from a womb. It includes those born from warmth and moisture, and those miraculously born. It includes those having a shape, and those without form. It includes those having ideas, and those without ideas. It even includes those neither with ideas nor without ideas. However many are called living beings in the realm of living beings, I will lead them all to complete freedom from sorrow in the expanse of freedom from sorrow with no group left over.' ^4-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-2]]

Countless living beings are led to complete freedom from sorrow in this way. But you must set the mind to think that no living being at all has been led to complete freedom from sorrow. ^4-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-3]]

Why is that? Because then they would not be called an awakening hero. ^4-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-4]]

Why is that? Because, Subhuti, if someone has the idea of a living being, the idea of a life, or the idea of a person, they are not called an awakening hero. ^4-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-5]]

Also, Subhuti, an awakening hero should practice giving without staying in a real object. ^4-5

They should practice giving without staying in anything. ^4-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-7]]

They should practice giving without staying in a shape. ^4-7

In the same way, they should practice giving without staying in a sound, a smell, a taste, a thing that is touched, or even a thing. ^4-8

Subhuti, an awakening hero should practice giving without staying even in the idea of a sign. ^4-9

Because there is no thing at all that the Thus-Gone One has taught. ^10-25

Why is that? Subhuti, as for what is called 'explaining the teaching', there is no teaching at all that you can hold onto as 'explaining the teaching'. ^10-27

## The Fruit Teaching Body ^11-0

The Buddhas should be seen as the true nature of things. ^11-3
