## Mind and Mental Factors ^10-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-1]]

Subhūti, the past mind is also unapprehendable. ^10-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-2]]

The future mind is also unapprehendable. ^10-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-3]]

It is because the present mind is also unapprehendable. ^10-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-4]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-5]]

If someone were to fill this trichiliocosm world system with the seven types of precious jewels and offer it as a gift, would that son or daughter of good family generate much merit on that basis? ^10-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-6]]

Subhūti replied: "Blessed One, it would be much." ^10-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-7]]

Sugata, it would be much. ^10-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-8]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, it is so. It is just so. ^10-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-9]]

That son or daughter of good family would generate a great heap of merit on that basis." ^10-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-10]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if a mass of merit were to become a mass of merit, the Tathāgata would not speak of it as a "mass of merit." ^10-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-11]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-12]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata should be viewed by means of the perfection of his form body? ^10-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-13]]

Subhūti replied, "No, Blessed One, it is not." ^10-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-14]]

The Tathāgata should not be viewed by means of the perfection of his form body. ^10-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-15]]

Why is that? Blessed One, it is because the perfection of the form body, as spoken of by the Tathāgata, is spoken of by the Tathāgata as the non-perfection of it. ^10-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-16]]

Therefore, it is called the perfection of the form body. ^10-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-17]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-18]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata should be viewed by means of the perfection of his marks?" ^10-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-19]]

Subhūti replied, "No, Blessed One, it is not so. ^10-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-20]]

The Tathāgata should not be viewed by means of the perfection of his marks. ^10-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-21]]

Why is that? Because the perfection of marks that the Tathāgata has spoken of, the Tathāgata has spoken of as being the non-perfection of marks. ^10-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-22]]

Therefore, it is called the perfection of marks. ^10-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-23]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-24]]

If you think that the Tathāgata considers, 'I have taught the Dharma,' Subhūti, do not view it in that way. ^10-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-25]]

It is because there is no phenomenon whatsoever that the Tathāgata has taught. ^10-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-26]]

Subhūti, if anyone were to say that the Tathāgata has taught a Dharma, that person would be speaking falsely and would be slandering me, because they have grasped at what is non-existent. ^10-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-27]]

Why is that? Subhūti, that which is called "teaching the Dharma" is such that there is no phenomenon whatsoever that can be apprehended as "teaching the Dharma" or "the Dharma that is taught." ^10-27

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-28]]

Then, the venerable Subhūti addressed the Blessed One in this way: ^10-28

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-29]]

Blessed One, in future times, will there be sentient beings who, upon hearing such an explanation of the Dharma as this, will give rise to true faith? ^10-29

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-30]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, those are neither sentient beings nor are they non-sentient beings." ^10-30

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-31]]

Why is that? Subhūti, it is because the Tathāgata has taught that those who are called sentient beings are not sentient beings. ^10-31

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-32]]

Therefore, they are called sentient beings. ^10-32

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-33]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-33

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-34]]

Do you think, Subhūti, that there is any dharma whatsoever by which the Tathāgata has fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment? ^10-34

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-35]]

The venerable Subhūti replied, "Blessed One, there is no dharma whatsoever by which the Tathāgata has fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment." ^10-35

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-36]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, it is so. It is just so." ^10-36

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-37]]

For there is not the slightest dharma to be conceptually observed or to exist. ^10-37

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-38]]

Therefore, it is called unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment. ^10-38

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-39]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, that dharma is equal; ^10-39

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-40]]

Since there is nothing whatsoever that is unequal or equal, it is called unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment. ^10-40

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-41]]

That unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment is equal in being without a self, without a sentient being, without a life-force, and without a person; ^10-41

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-42]]

it is perfectly and completely awakened through all wholesome dharmas. ^10-42

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-43]]

Subhūti, as for what are called "wholesome dharmas," the Tathāgata has taught that they are devoid of the nature of dharmas; ^10-43

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-44]]

therefore, they are called "wholesome dharmas." ^10-44

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-45]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if any son or daughter of noble family were to gather a heap of the seven precious substances equal in size to as many Mount Merus, the kings of mountains, as there are in the trichiliocosm, and give them as a gift, and if another person were to take from this Perfection of Wisdom even just a four-line stanza and teach it to others, Subhūti, that former heap of merit would not approach even a hundredth part of the latter, nor would it bear comparison, even up to the point of calculation. ^10-45

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-46]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-46

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-47]]

If you think that the Tathāgata considers, "I have liberated sentient beings," Subhūti, do not view it in that way. ^10-47

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-48]]

Why is that? Subhūti, it is because there are no sentient beings whatsoever that have been liberated by the Tathāgata. ^10-48

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-49]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to liberate any sentient being whatsoever, that would be the Tathāgata grasping at a self. ^10-49

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-50]]

It would be grasping at a sentient being, grasping at a life-force, and grasping at a person. ^10-50

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-51]]

Subhūti, what is called "grasping at a self" has been declared by the Tathāgata to be "non-grasping," yet it is grasped by immature ordinary beings. ^10-51

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-52]]

Subhūti, as for what are called "immature ordinary beings," the Tathāgata has declared them to be non-beings. ^10-52

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-53]]

Therefore, they are called "immature ordinary beings." ^10-53

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-54]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-54

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-55]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata should be seen by means of his perfect marks? ^10-55

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-56]]

Subhūti replied, "No, Blessed One, it is not so." ^10-56

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-57]]

"The Tathāgata should not be seen by means of his perfect marks." ^10-57

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-58]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, it is so. It is just so. ^10-58

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-59]]

The Tathāgata should not be seen by means of his perfect marks. ^10-59

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-60]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to be seen by means of his perfect marks, then a wheel-turning monarch would also be a Tathāgata." ^10-60

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-61]]

Therefore, the Tathāgata should not be seen by means of his perfect marks. ^10-61

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-62]]

Then, the venerable Subhūti addressed the Blessed One in this way: ^10-62

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-63]]

"Blessed One, as I understand the meaning of what the Blessed One has said, the Tathāgata should not be seen by means of his perfect marks." ^10-63

## The Fruition of the Dharmakāya ^11-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-1]]

Those who see me as form, those who perceive me as sound, have entered upon a mistaken path; ^11-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-2]]

Those people do not see me. ^11-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-3]]

The Buddhas are to be seen as the nature of reality. ^11-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-4]]

The guides are the Dharmakāya; since the nature of reality is not an object to be known, it cannot be cognized. ^11-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-5]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^11-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-6]]

If you think that the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, has fully awakened to perfect enlightenment by means of the perfection of marks, Subhūti, you should not view it in that way. ^11-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-7]]

Subhūti, there is no Tathāgata, Arhat, perfectly complete Buddha who has fully awakened to perfect enlightenment by means of the perfection of marks. ^11-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-8]]

Subhūti, if you think that those who have correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle hold the view that any phenomenon is destroyed or designated as cut off, Subhūti, you should not view it in that way. ^11-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-9]]

Those who have correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle do not hold that any phenomenon is destroyed or designated as cut off. ^11-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-10]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if a son or daughter of noble family were to fill as many world systems as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges with the seven types of precious jewels and give them as a gift, and if a bodhisattva were to gain patience in the realization that phenomena are without self and unborn, then that bodhisattva would generate a mass of merit far greater, immeasurable, and incalculable than that. ^11-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-11]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bodhisattva should not take hold of a mass of merit. ^11-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-12]]

The venerable Subhūti asked, "Blessed One, should a bodhisattva not take hold of a mass of merit?" ^11-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-13]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, one may indeed take hold of it, but one should not take hold of it wrongly. ^11-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-14]]

Therefore, it is called 'taking hold'." ^11-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-15]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if anyone were to say that the Tathāgata comes or goes, stands, sits, or lies down, that person does not understand the meaning of what I have taught. ^11-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-16]]

Why is that? Subhūti, the one called Tathāgata does not go anywhere, nor does he come from anywhere. ^11-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-17]]

Therefore, he is called the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha. ^11-17

## The Most Minute Particles ^12-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-1]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if a noble son or a noble daughter were to take all the earth-dust particles in the trichiliocosm world-system and reduce them to a collection of the finest atoms, what do you think, Subhūti? Would that collection of the finest atoms be numerous? ^12-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-2]]

Subhūti replied, "It is so, Bhagavan. ^12-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-3]]

That collection of the finest atoms would be numerous." ^12-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-4]]

Why is that? Bhagavan, if a collection of the finest atoms were to exist, the Bhagavan would not have spoken of a "collection of the finest atoms." ^12-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-5]]

Why is that? It is because what the Bhagavan has spoken of as a "collection of the finest atoms," the Tathāgata has spoken of as not being a collection. ^12-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-6]]

Therefore, it is called a "collection of the finest atoms." ^12-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-7]]

What the Tathāgata has spoken of as a "three-thousandfold great thousand world system," the Tathāgata has spoken of as not being a world system. ^12-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-8]]

Therefore, it is called a "three-thousandfold great thousand world system." ^12-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-9]]

Why is that? Bhagavan, if a world system were to exist, it would be grasping at a totality. ^12-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-10]]

What the Tathāgata has spoken of as grasping at a totality, the Tathāgata has spoken of as not being a grasping, and for that reason it is so. ^12-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-11]]

Therefore, it is called "grasping at a totality." ^12-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-12]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, grasping at a totality is merely a conventional designation." ^12-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-13]]

That phenomenon is inexpressible, yet it is grasped by immature ordinary beings. ^12-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-14]]

Subhūti, if someone were to say, "The Tathāgata has spoken of a view of a self," ^12-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-15]]

and were to say, "The Tathāgata has spoken of a view of a sentient being, a view of a life-force, and a view of a person," would that person be speaking correctly? ^12-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-16]]

Subhūti replied, "No, Blessed One, it is not." ^12-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-17]]

"It is not so, Sugata." ^12-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-18]]

"Why is that? Because, Blessed One, what the Tathāgata has spoken of as a view of a self, the Tathāgata has spoken of as a non-view; that is why it is called a view of a self." ^12-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-19]]

Therefore, it is called a view of a self. ^12-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-20]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, in this regard, one who has correctly entered the path of the bodhisattva should know, see, and resolve to understand all phenomena in this way: ^12-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-21]]

"They should know how to abide without dwelling on the perception of phenomena." ^12-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-22]]

Why is that? Subhūti, the so-called "perception of phenomena" is spoken of by the Tathāgata as being without perception. ^12-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-23]]

Therefore, it is called the perception of phenomena, the perception of phenomena. ^12-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-24]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, if a bodhisattva mahāsattva were to fill immeasurable and incalculable world systems with the seven types of precious substances and give them as a gift, compared to that, if a son or daughter of noble family were to take up, bear, read, master, or extensively and correctly teach to others even just a four-line verse from this perfection of wisdom, they would generate much greater, incalculable, and immeasurable merit. ^12-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-25]]

How does one perfectly and thoroughly teach? It is by perfectly and thoroughly teaching in the same way that one does not perfectly and thoroughly teach. ^12-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-26]]

Therefore, it is called perfectly and thoroughly teaching. ^12-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-27]]

Like a star, a visual fault, a lamp,
A magical illusion, a dewdrop, a bubble,
A dream, a flash of lightning, and a cloud—
Thus should one view all conditioned phenomena. ^12-27
