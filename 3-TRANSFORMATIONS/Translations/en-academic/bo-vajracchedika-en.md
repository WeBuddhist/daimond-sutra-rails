## Generating the Mind of Awakening ^4-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-1]]

Subhūti, here one who has correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle should think in this way: 'However many sentient beings are subsumed by the subsumption of sentient beings—whether born from eggs, or born from a womb, or born from warmth and moisture, or miraculously born, or having form, or without form, or having perception, or neither with perception nor without perception—however many are designated by conceptual designation as sentient beings in the realm of sentient beings, all those I shall lead to complete nirvāṇa in the expanse of nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates.' ^4-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-2]]

Even though immeasurable sentient beings are thus led to complete nirvāṇa, one should generate the mind thinking that no sentient being whatsoever has been led to complete nirvāṇa. ^4-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-3]]

Why is that? Because they would not be called a bodhisattva. ^4-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-4]]

Why is that? Because, Subhūti, if one engages in the perception of a sentient being, engages in the perception of a life-force, or engages in the perception of a person, they are not to be called a bodhisattva. ^4-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-5]]

Moreover, Subhūti, a bodhisattva should practice generosity without abiding in an entity. ^4-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-6]]

They should practice generosity without abiding in anything. ^4-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-7]]

They should practice generosity without abiding in form. ^4-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-8]]

Likewise, generosity should be practiced without abiding in sounds, smells, tastes, tangible objects, or even phenomena. ^4-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-9]]

Subhūti, a bodhisattva should practice generosity in such a way that they are without abiding even in the perception of a sign. ^4-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^4-10]]

Why is that? ^4-10

This is because the present mind, too, is unapprehendable. ^10-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-4]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-5]]

If someone were to completely fill this three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system with the seven precious substances and give a gift, would that son of noble family or daughter of noble family produce much merit on that basis? ^10-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-6]]

Subhūti replied: "Blessed One, it is much. ^10-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-7]]

Sugata, it is much." ^10-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-8]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, it is so; it is so. ^10-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-9]]

That son of noble family or daughter of noble family would produce a great mass of merit on that basis. ^10-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-10]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if a mass of merit were a mass of merit, the Tathāgata would not speak of "a mass of merit, a mass of merit". ^10-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-11]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-12]]

Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfect accomplishment of the form body? ^10-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-13]]

Subhūti said: Blessed One, it is not so. ^10-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-14]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfect accomplishment of the form body. ^10-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-15]]

Why is that? Blessed One, it is because what is called the perfect accomplishment of the form body has been spoken of by the Tathāgata as no perfect accomplishment. ^10-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-16]]

Therefore it is called the perfect accomplishment of the form body. ^10-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-17]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-18]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of marks? ^10-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-19]]

Subhūti replied: Blessed One, it is not so. ^10-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-20]]

The Tathāgata is not to be viewed by the perfection of marks. ^10-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-21]]

Why is that? Because whatever perfection of marks the Tathāgata has spoken of, that has been spoken of by the Tathāgata as no perfection of marks. ^10-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-22]]

Therefore, it is called the perfection of marks. ^10-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-23]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-24]]

If you think that the Tathāgata thinks, 'I have taught the Dharma,' Subhūti, do not view it in that way. ^10-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-25]]

Because there is no phenomenon whatsoever that the Tathāgata has taught. ^10-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-26]]

Subhūti, if anyone were to say this: 'The Tathāgata has taught the Dharma,' Subhūti, they would slander me by a mistaken grasping of what does not exist. ^10-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-27]]

Why is that? Subhūti, as for what is called 'teaching the Dharma', it is because there is no Dharma whatsoever that can be apprehended as 'teaching the Dharma'. ^10-27

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-28]]

Then the venerable Subhūti said this to the Blessed One: ^10-28

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-29]]

“Blessed One, in the future, will there be any sentient beings who, having heard such an explanation of the Dharma, will come to truly believe it?” ^10-29

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-30]]

The Blessed One said: “Subhūti, they are neither sentient beings, nor are they not sentient beings. ^10-30

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-31]]

Why is that? Subhūti, as for what are called 'sentient beings,' it is because the Tathāgata has taught that they are not sentient beings.” ^10-31

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-32]]

Therefore, they are called sentient beings. ^10-32

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-33]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-33

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-34]]

Do you think there is any phenomenon whatsoever that the Tathāgata has fully awakened to as the unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening? ^10-34

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-35]]

The venerable Subhūti said, Blessed One, there is no phenomenon whatsoever that the Tathāgata has fully awakened to as the unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. ^10-35

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-36]]

The Blessed One said, Subhūti, it is so. It is just so. ^10-36

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-37]]

Therein, not even the slightest phenomenon is apprehended or exists. ^10-37

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-38]]

Therefore, it is called unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. ^10-38

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-39]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, that phenomenon is equal; ^10-39

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-40]]

Since there is nothing whatsoever unequal or equal in it, therefore it is called unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. ^10-40

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-41]]

That unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening is equal in being without a self, without a sentient being, without a life-force, and without a person; ^10-41

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-42]]

it is fully awakened to by means of all virtuous phenomena. ^10-42

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-43]]

Subhūti, as for what are called 'virtuous phenomena', the Tathāgata has taught them to be exactly non-phenomena; ^10-43

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-44]]

Therefore, they are called 'virtuous phenomena'. ^10-44

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-45]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, suppose any son of noble family or daughter of noble family brought together masses of the seven precious substances equal to however many kings of mountains, Mount Sumerus, there are in the three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system, and gave them as a gift; and suppose someone took up even just a single four-line verse from this perfection of wisdom and taught it to others. Subhūti, that former mass of merit does not come near even a hundredth part of the latter, up to not bearing comparison even as a cause. ^10-45

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-46]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-46

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-47]]

If you think that the Tathāgata thinks, 'I have liberated sentient beings,' Subhūti, it should not be viewed like that. ^10-47

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-48]]

Why is that? Because, Subhūti, there are no sentient beings whatsoever who have been liberated by the Tathāgata. ^10-48

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-49]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to liberate any sentient being whatsoever, that itself would be grasping at a self by the Tathāgata. ^10-49

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-50]]

it would be grasping at a sentient being, grasping at a life-force, and grasping at a person. ^10-50

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-51]]

Subhūti, as for what is called grasping at a self, the Tathāgata has spoken of it as non-grasping; yet it is grasped by childish ordinary beings. ^10-51

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-52]]

Subhūti, as for what are called childish ordinary beings, the Tathāgata has spoken of them as exactly non-beings; ^10-52

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-53]]

therefore they are called childish ordinary beings. ^10-53

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-54]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-54

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-55]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of marks? ^10-55

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-56]]

Subhūti replied, Blessed One, it is not so. ^10-56

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-57]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of marks. ^10-57

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-58]]

The Blessed One said, Subhūti, it is so. It is just so. ^10-58

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-59]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of marks; ^10-59

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-60]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to be seen by the perfection of marks, a wheel-turning monarch would also be a Tathāgata. ^10-60

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-61]]

Therefore, the Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of marks. ^10-61

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-62]]

Then the venerable Subhūti spoke thus to the Blessed One: ^10-62

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-63]]

"Blessed One, as I understand the meaning of what the Blessed One has spoken, the Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of marks." ^10-63

## The Fruit Dharmakāya ^11-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-1]]

Those who see me as form, those who know me as sound, have entered upon a wrong path; ^11-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-2]]

Those people do not see me. ^11-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-3]]

The Buddhas are to be seen as the nature of phenomena. ^11-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-4]]

The guides are the Dharmakāya. Since the nature of phenomena is not an object to be known, it cannot be cognized. ^11-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-5]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^11-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-6]]

If you think that the Tathāgata, the arhat, has fully awakened to perfectly complete awakening by means of the perfection of marks, Subhūti, you should not view it like that. ^11-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-7]]

Subhūti, the Tathāgata, the arhat, has not fully awakened to perfectly complete awakening by means of the perfection of marks. ^11-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-8]]

Subhūti, if you think that those who have correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle have destroyed some phenomenon or designated it as an annihilation, Subhūti, you should not view it like that. ^11-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-9]]

Those who have correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle do not designate any phenomenon as destroyed or as an annihilation. ^11-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-10]]

Moreover, Subhūti, compared to a son of noble family or daughter of noble family who fills world systems as numerous as the sand grains of the Gaṅgā river with the seven precious substances and gives them as a gift, if a bodhisattva attains patience in this Dharma discourse being without self and unborn, he generates from that basis a mass of merit vastly greater, incalculable, and immeasurable. ^11-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-11]]

Moreover, Subhūti, a bodhisattva should not take hold of a mass of merit. ^11-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-12]]

The venerable Subhūti asked, "Blessed One, should a bodhisattva not take hold of a mass of merit?" ^11-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-13]]

The Blessed One replied, "Subhūti, one should indeed take hold of it, but one should not take hold of it wrongly; ^11-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-14]]

Therefore, it is called 'taking hold'. ^11-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-15]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if anyone were to say, "The Tathāgata goes or comes, stands, sits, or lies down," that person does not understand the meaning of what I have explained. ^11-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-16]]

Why is that? Subhūti, it is because the one called "Tathāgata" has not gone anywhere and has not come from anywhere. ^11-16

Therefore he is called the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha. ^11-17

## Subtle Particles ^12-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-1]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if any son of noble family or daughter of noble family were to grind as many particles of earth as there are in the three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system into powder so that they become, for example, like a collection of subtle particles, what do you think, Subhūti, would that collection of subtle particles be numerous? ^12-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-2]]

Subhūti replied, "Blessed One, it is so. ^12-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-3]]

That collection of subtle particles would be numerous. ^12-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-4]]

Why is that? Because, Blessed One, if a collection of subtle particles existed, the Blessed One would not have spoken of a 'collection of subtle particles'. ^12-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-5]]

Why is that? It is because what the Blessed One has spoken of as a 'collection of subtle particles', the Tathāgata has spoken of as no collection. ^12-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-6]]

Therefore, it is called a 'collection of subtle particles.' ^12-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-7]]

What the Tathāgata has spoken of as a 'three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system,' the Tathāgata has spoken of as not being a system; ^12-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-8]]

Therefore, it is called a 'three-thousandfold great thousandfold world system.' ^12-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-9]]

Why is that? Blessed One, because if a world system existed, that itself would be grasping at a totality. ^12-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-10]]

Because what the Tathāgata has spoken of as grasping at a totality, the Tathāgata has spoken of as no grasping, ^12-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-11]]

Therefore, it is called "grasping at a totality." ^12-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-12]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, grasping at a totality is itself a conventional designation; ^12-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-13]]

that phenomenon is inexpressible, yet it is grasped by childish ordinary beings. ^12-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-14]]

Subhūti, if someone were to say: The Tathāgata has spoken of a view of a self, ^12-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-15]]

If someone were to say, "The Tathāgata has spoken of the view of a sentient being, the view of a life-force, and the view of a person," would they be speaking correctly? ^12-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-16]]

Subhūti replied, Blessed One, it is not so. ^12-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-17]]

Sugata, it is not so. ^12-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-18]]

Why is that? Because, Blessed One, what the Tathāgata has spoken of as the view of a self, the Tathāgata has spoken of as no view. ^12-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-19]]

Therefore, it is called the view of a self. ^12-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-20]]

The Blessed One said, Subhūti, in this regard, one who has correctly entered the bodhisattva vehicle should know, see, and resolve upon all phenomena in this way: ^12-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-21]]

They should be known in such a way that one does not abide in the perception of a phenomenon. ^12-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-22]]

Why is that? Subhūti, as for what is called 'perception of a phenomenon, perception of a phenomenon,' the Tathāgata has spoken of it as no perception. ^12-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-23]]

Therefore it is called 'perception of a phenomenon, perception of a phenomenon'. ^12-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-24]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, compared to a bodhisattva great being who fills immeasurable and incalculable world systems with the seven precious substances and gives them as a gift, if a son of noble family or daughter of noble family writes down, takes up, bears, reads, masters, or extensively and perfectly teaches to others even just a four-line verse from this perfection of wisdom, they will produce much more merit, incalculable and immeasurable, on that basis. ^12-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-25]]

How does one perfectly teach it? Just as one does not perfectly teach it, in that way one perfectly teaches it. ^12-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-26]]

Therefore it is called perfectly teaching. ^12-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-27]]

Like a star, a visual aberration, a lamp, an illusion, a dewdrop, a bubble, a dream, lightning, and a cloud—thus should one view all conditioned phenomena. ^12-27
