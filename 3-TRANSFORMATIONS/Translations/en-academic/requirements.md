# Requirements — Diamond Sutra, English, academic

## Audience and register
Students and practitioners reading a careful study translation; university readers of Buddhist texts (see `audience.md`). Faithful, precise, readable English prose; no archaism.

## Source and grounding
- Translate from the Tibetan. Use the aligned Sanskrit and the aligned commentary passages to settle the meaning of each segment; the two machine drafts (DharmaMitra, Gemini) may be consulted but are not authorities.
- Translate the whole segment, in order: no omissions, no additions, no summary.
- Never add explanations, glosses or notes in the translation itself.

## Vocabulary
- Every term in `termbase.md` is rendered with its locked academic rendering, inflected for number and grammar as needed. Where a term has several senses, the sense assigned to that occurrence is used.
- Sanskrit kept only where the termbase keeps it, with IAST diacritics; proper names in IAST (Subhūti, Śrāvastī).

## Style
- Standard modern English punctuation; sentences may be long where the Tibetan argument requires, but each must parse cleanly.
- Keep the sutra's repetitions and formulae: a recurring Tibetan formula is translated the same way every time.
- Direct speech in quotation marks only where the Tibetan marks speech (ཞེས་...).
- Headings: translate the Tibetan heading literally and concisely.
