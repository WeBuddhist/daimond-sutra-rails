# Extensive Commentary on the Seven Topics of the Noble Blessed Perfection of Wisdom Diamond Cutter by Master Vasubandhu ^0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^0-1]]

The Extensive Commentary on the Seven Points of the Noble Bhagavatī Prajñāpāramitā Vajracchedikā ^0-1

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^0-2]]

In the Indian language: Āryabhagavatīprajñāpāramitāvajracchedikāyāḥ saptadārthaṭīkā. In the Tibetan language: The Extensive Commentary on the Seven Points of the Noble Bhagavatī Prajñāpāramitā Vajracchedikā. Homage to the noble prajñāpāramitā! To she who generates the incomparable qualities of a Buddha, who is the nursing mother of the dharmadhātu, who is the indivisible vajra accomplishing the aims of others, who by being grasped gives birth to all noble ones, who possesses the power of being merely designated by name, the foremost prajñāpāramitā invoked as the Vajra: whatever its meaning is according to the speech of whoever taught it, having heard it, I too will strive to grasp it. In order to understand those aspects of hers, I bow to her with the crown of my head above all entities. In order to grasp that meaning composed and explained by others, I too will make an effort to explain it. ^0-2

## Commentary on the Body of the Sutra ^1-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-1]]

This prajñāpāramitā is established through the establishment of seven points of meaning. The seven points of meaning are as follows: the uninterrupted continuity of the lineage of the Buddhas, the characteristics of application, its support, the antidotes, non-degeneration, the stages, and the reason for the name. Because these seven points of meaning are the supports of the prajñāpāramitā, they are to be called "points of meaning." One should know that by the six points of meaning the activities of a bodhisattva are completely fulfilled, and by the seventh the purpose of this dharma-discourse is completely taught. ^1-1

### Teaching the Uninterrupted Lineage of the Buddhas ^1-1-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-2]]

This prajñāpāramitā is also undertaken starting from the uninterrupted continuity of the lineage of the Buddhas, that is, how the lineage of the Buddhas will become uninterrupted. ^1-2

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-3]]

This very point is detailed at the very beginning by the Elder Subhūti: "It is wonderful to whatever extent the Bhagavat Tathāgata has benefited the great bodhisattvas with benefit." In that regard, "benefit" is the complete ripening of the bodhisattvas at the time of manifestly perfect awakening, that is, turning the wheel of dharma, because of firmly establishing the bodhisattvas in the dharmatā within the five points of meaning. "Entrustment" is benefiting those who are not completely ripened, for the purpose of making them abide firmly at the time of completely passing into nirvāṇa; thus, by "benefit" and "entrustment", the uninterrupted continuity of the lineage is completely taught. ^1-3

### Teaching the Characteristics of Application ^1-2-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-4]]

The characteristics of application are "how should a bodhisattva abide?" and so forth. In that regard, "should abide" is by aspiration and prayer. "Should practice" is by entering into meditative absorption in yoga. "Should completely subdue the mind" is by cutting off distraction. In that regard, "aspiration" is manifest desire, and "prayer" is the mental formation through thinking upon the object of manifest desire. "Entering into meditative absorption in yoga" is the non-conceptual samādhi. "Cutting off distraction" is turning back from directing toward what is not meditative absorption and establishing it right in that state. By the first, the path is completely grasped; by the second, the path is completed; and by the third, the path is completely taught as non-degenerating. ^1-4

### Teaching the Support of the Characteristics of Application ^1-3-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-5]]

Its support is of that very characteristic of application; and whatever the support is, it should also be known in eighteen aspects. They are as follows: generation of the mind, application to the pāramitās, aspiration to attain the form body, aspiration to attain the dharma body, the absence of manifest pride in realizing the distinction of cultivation, pleasing the emergence of Buddhas, prayer for the complete purity of the field, completely ripening sentient beings, solitude from attachment to the essenceless treatises of outsiders, application free from grasping as a whole at the bodies of form sentient beings, offering to and serving the Tathāgata, solitude from unundertaken gain and honor having turned back from exertion in the complete torment of fatigue, the endurance of suffering, solitude from tasting the flavor of samādhi, solitude from grasping with the thought "I am" at the time of manifest realization, completely seeking instruction, manifest realization, and completely seeking the stage of a Buddha above that. These eighteen aspects are the support, upon which a bodhisattva should abide, should practice, and should completely subdue the mind. ^1-5

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-6]]

Having generated the mind in order to completely pass all sentient beings beyond sorrow, one completely applies oneself to the pāramitās and generates the aspiration to realize the form body or dharma body of the Tathāgata. One makes the mind completely solitary from the obstacles to manifest realization, and having cleared away manifest pride, in order to reach the culmination, one completely seeks the instruction for the manifest realization of the completely solitary mind. From that, one manifestly realizes, and above that one completely seeks the stage of a Buddha; thus, the sequence of these and those should be known. In that regard, with respect to the generation of the mind, it is whatever was taught such as, "A bodhisattva should generate the mind in this way." With respect to the application to the pāramitās, it was taught, "A bodhisattva should give gifts without abiding in entities." With respect to the aspiration to attain the form body, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Should the Tathāgata be seen by the perfection of marks?" The dharma body is also of two aspects: the teaching dharma body and the realization dharma body. In that regard, the realization dharma body is also of two aspects: the nature of pristine awareness and the nature of merit. ^1-6

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-7]]

In that regard, with respect to the aspiration to attain the teaching dharma body, it was taught such as, "Bhagavat, will there be any sentient beings arising in the future time?" The perception of the unmistakable meaning should also be known here as the correct perception; it is the perception thinking "these are correct" due to manifest adherence to the non-literal meaning. With respect to the aspiration to attain the dharma body of the nature of pristine awareness, it was taught such as, "Is there any dharma by which the Tathāgata manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening?" With respect to the aspiration to attain the dharma body of the nature of merit, it was taught such as, "If anyone, this trichiliocosm and great chiliocosm..." With respect to the absence of manifest pride in realizing the distinction of cultivation, it was taught such as, "Would a stream-enterer think this..." With respect to pleasing the emergence of Buddhas, it was taught such as, "Is there any dharma whatever that the Tathāgata took from the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha Dīpaṃkara?" ^1-7

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-8]]

With respect to the prayer for the complete purity of the field, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, if any bodhisattva says this: 'I will accomplish the arrays of the field'..." With respect to completely ripening sentient beings, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, it is like this: for example, if a person..." With respect to solitude from attachment to the essenceless treatises of outsiders, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? However many grains of sand there are in the river Gaṅgā, if there were that many rivers Gaṅgā..." With respect to application free from grasping as a whole at the bodies of form sentient beings, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, if any son of noble family or daughter of noble family... however many particles of earth there are in the trichiliocosm and great chiliocosm..." With respect to offering to and serving the Tathāgata, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Should the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha be seen by the thirty-two marks of a great man?" With respect to solitude from unundertaken gain and honor having turned back from exertion in fatigue and complete torment, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, if any man or woman gives up bodies as numerous as the grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā..." In that regard, fatigue is of the body and complete torment is of the mind; with respect to the endurance of unundertaken suffering having turned back from exertion in those, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, whatever is the pāramitā of the Tathāgata..." With respect to solitude from tasting the flavor of samādhi, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, if any man or woman... bodies as numerous as the grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā..." ^1-8

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-9]]

With respect to solitude from grasping with the thought 'I am' at the time of manifest realization, it was taught such as, "Bhagavat, how should a bodhisattva abide?" With respect to completely seeking instruction, it was taught such as, "Is there any dharma whatever by which the Tathāgata manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening from the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara?" With respect to manifest realization, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, it is like this: for example, if a person is possessed of a body, a great body..." In that regard, being possessed of a body is the attainment of a body, because of attaining the extremely ultimate transformation of state in which the body is perfect. A great body is because of appropriating the bodies of all sentient beings. Above that, it should be known that one completely seeks the stage of a Buddha; that stage of a Buddha, moreover, is the transformation of state completely subsumed by the six perfections. The six perfections are like this: the perfection of the complete purity of the field, the perfection of the complete purity of unsurpassed seeing and pristine awareness, the perfection of mastery over the aggregate of merit, the perfection of the body, the perfection of speech, and the perfection of mind. ^1-9

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-10]]

That perfection of mind, moreover, should be known as the establishments of mindfulness, manifest perfect awakening, the designation as a dharma of great meaning, the designation as an instruction of great meaning, being completely subsumed by the dharma body, the non-abiding in nirvāṇa and saṃsāra, and the complete purity of moving and abiding; the complete purity of moving and abiding, moreover, should be known as the deportment of moving and abiding, the moving and abiding of the mastery of meditative cultivation on form and name, and the moving and abiding without complete affliction. The absence of complete affliction, moreover, should be known as the absence of complete affliction regarding the teacher and the absence of complete affliction of saṃsāra. In that regard, with respect to the perfection of the complete purity of the field, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, if any bodhisattva says this: 'I will accomplish the arrays of the field'..." With respect to the perfection of the complete purity of unsurpassed seeing, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Does the Tathāgata possess the fleshly eye?" With respect to the perfection of the complete purity of unsurpassed pristine awareness, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? However many grains of sand there are in the river Gaṅgā..." ^1-10

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-11]]

With respect to mastery over the aggregate of merit, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? The trichiliocosm and great chiliocosm..." With respect to the perfection of the body of minor marks, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Should the Tathāgata be seen by the complete accomplishment of the form body?" With respect to the perfection of the body of marks, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Should the Tathāgata be seen by the perfection of the marks?" With respect to the perfection of speech, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Does the Tathāgata think this: 'I have taught the dharma'?" With respect to the perfection of mind as the establishments of mindfulness, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, they are neither sentient beings nor non-sentient beings." With respect to manifest perfect awakening, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Is there any dharma whatever by which the Tathāgata manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening?" With respect to the designation as a dharma of great meaning, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, however many Sumerus, kings of mountains, there are in the trichiliocosm and great chiliocosm..." ^1-11

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-12]]

With respect to the designation as an instruction of great meaning, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Does the Tathāgata think this: 'I have liberated sentient beings'?" With respect to being completely subsumed by the dharma body, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, what do you think? Should the Tathāgata be seen by the perfection of the marks?" As for the saying, "Subhūti, what do you think? Do you think, 'The Tathāgata manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening by the perfection of the marks'?"—moreover, what is the meaning? The meaning is to completely clarify that awakening, having the nature of the perfection of marks, also has the perfection of marks as its cause. With respect to the non-abiding in nirvāṇa, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, do you think this: 'Is some dharma destroyed by those who have correctly entered into the vehicle of the bodhisattvas'?" With respect to the non-abiding in saṃsāra, it was taught such as, "Subhūti, should a bodhisattva not completely grasp the aggregate of merit?" "What is to be completely grasped, Subhūti, is not to be grasped" means that it is not to be grasped by the path, and just like the aggregate of merit, these also will not truly arise. ^1-12

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-13]]

With respect to the complete purity of moving and abiding being the moving and abiding of deportment, it was taught such as, "If anyone [says that] the Tathāgata goes..." With respect to the moving and abiding by mastery over the meditative cultivation on form and name, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, if any son of noble family or daughter of noble family... however many particles of earth there are in the trichiliocosm and great chiliocosm..." From among the movings and abidings without complete affliction of saṃsāra, with respect to the absence of complete affliction regarding the teacher, it was taught such as, "Moreover, Subhūti, any bodhisattva... immeasurable and incalculable world systems..." With respect to the absence of complete affliction of saṃsāra, by this verse: "Like a star, an impairment of vision, a lamp, an illusion, dew, a bubble, a dream, lightning, and a cloud—thus should conditioned things be viewed," the four aspects of the characteristics of conditioned things were taught: the characteristic of the nature, the characteristic of the experience of taste that is not a basis for attachment, the characteristic of following after faults, and the characteristic of following after definitive emergence. ^1-13

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-14]]

In that regard, as for the characteristic of the nature: the view accompanied by marks is consciousness. In that regard, marks should be viewed as being like a star. By what meaning? Because they appear in the darkness of ignorance, and because, despite existing in the light of pristine awareness, they go to non-appearance. Viewing as persons and dharmas should be viewed as being like someone with an impairment of vision. By what meaning? Because it apprehends objects that have not come to exist. Consciousness should be viewed as similar to a lamp. By what meaning? Because it burns in dependence upon the fuel of the oil of craving. In that regard, as for the characteristic of the experience of taste that is not a basis for attachment, it is the experiencing of objects that are to be posited as mistaken; that should be viewed as being like an illusion. By what meaning? Because it is a mistaken appearance. In that regard, as for the characteristic of following after faults, it is because of following after impermanence and also because of following after suffering. In that regard, precisely by being like dew, it completely clarified the reality of the body being connected with momentary impermanence. Precisely by being like a bubble, the very connection of feelings with suffering is like a bubble. Having said, 'Whatever is felt, this is suffering here,' feelings are suffering in whatever way is appropriate through the three aspects of suffering; in that regard, what arises from birth is the suffering of suffering itself. ^1-14

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-15]]

What arises from separation is the suffering of change itself. What arises from correct connection and non-separation is the suffering of conditioning itself. That, moreover, is posited mainly by the feeling that is neither suffering nor happiness in the four dhyānas and the formless [realms]. In that regard, as for the characteristic of following after definitive emergence: because of definitive emergence by observing the connection with the selflessness of persons and dharmas, selflessness is explained as the characteristic of definitive emergence. In that regard, as for following after, conditioned things of the past and so forth are completely clarified as being like a dream and so forth. In that regard, past conditioned things are like a dream precisely because they abide in memory. Present ones are like lightning precisely because they do not abide for a long time. Future ones are like a cloud precisely because their seeds, which are bad states, obscure the space-like mind. Thus, in order to completely know the engagement of all conditioned things of the three times, saying, "These should be realized as selfless," the characteristic of following after definitive emergence is completely clearly taught. As for the supports of those various things, they are supports that clarify manifest resemblance, namely: the support of complete upholding, the support of the complete purity of the perfections, the support of aspiration, the support of isolation from hindrances, the support of the complete purity of intention, the support of culmination, the support of vastness, and the support of profundity. ^1-15

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-16]]

The support of completely upholding is the generation of the mind [of awakening]. The support of the complete purity of the perfections is the application of the perfections. The support of aspiration is making one obtain the form and dharma bodies. The support of isolation from hindrances is the remaining twelve. The support of the complete purity of intention is manifest realization. The support of culmination is the complete seeking of the ground of a Buddha above that. The supports of vastness and profundity are all of them. First of all, with regard to the support, a bodhisattva should generate the mind [of awakening]: such as, "Whatever sentient beings there are..." This is vast; moreover, [it was taught] such as, "If a bodhisattva engages in the perception of a sentient being..." This is profound and is also the second support. It was taught such as, "Any bodhisattva should give gifts without abiding in entities"; this is profound. Moreover, it was taught such as, "That aggregate of merit is not easy to take the measure of"; this is vast. Thus, regarding the other supports as well, they should be known as profound and vast in whatever way is appropriate; the supports have been explained. ^1-16

### Teaching the Antidotes ^1-4-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-17]]

What is the antidote? That itself should be known as the antidote to the two: the characteristic of application and the engagement of its support. Wrong practice is through two aspects, and also the views of the transitory collection. When practicing correctly, 'view' is here asserted to be conceptualization. First of all, regarding the support, a bodhisattva should generate the mind [of awakening] thus: 'Whatever sentient beings there are...' — this is the antidote to wrong practice. Not generating the mind in that way becomes wrong practice. What was taught further, 'If a bodhisattva engages in the perception of a sentient being...' — this is the correct view, and the antidote to correct practice. These too are adherence through conceptualization, and are to be abandoned by a bodhisattva. 'Sentient beings should be completely passed beyond sorrow' is the second; what was taught, 'Gifts should be given,' is the antidote to wrong practice; if one does not give, a bodhisattva will have entered into wrongness. What was further taught, 'Gifts should be given without abiding in entities,' is the correct view, and is the antidote to correct practice. These too are adherence through conceptualization, and are to be abandoned by a bodhisattva, and gifts should be given. ^1-17

### Teaching Non-Degeneration ^1-5-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-18]]

What is non-deterioration? It is the complete abandonment of the two extremes. What are the extremes of this? They are the extreme of superimposition and the extreme of deprecation. Any adherence to dharmas in accordance with the words as having an un-imputed essence is the extreme of superimposition. Any adherence to even the basis of the selflessness of dharmas as a non-entity is the extreme of deprecation. In that regard, what was taught by saying, "Bhagavān, that very aggregate of merit is not an aggregate," refutes the extreme of superimposition. Because that aggregate of merit does not exist, whatever exists in accordance with the words exists by an imputed essence; moreover, therefore, what was taught by the Tathāgata saying, "aggregate of merit," refutes the extreme of deprecation. That essence which is not in accordance with the words exists to a slight extent; whatever is an inexpressible entity completely clarifies what was taught by the Tathāgata as "aggregate of merit." Similarly, regarding "Subhūti, the dharmas of a Buddha, the Buddhas...", by saying "the Tathāgata has taught that those dharmas of a Buddha do not exist," the extreme of superimposition is refuted; therefore, by saying "dharmas of a Buddha," the deterioration of deprecation is refuted. In that regard, by saying "the Tathāgata has taught that those dharmas of a Buddha do not exist," the uncommon meaning itself is completely clarified; therefore, expressing "dharmas of a Buddha" is precisely endowed with meaning. If the dharmas of a Buddha did not exist by the essence exactly as they were taught, and if the Tathāgata were to teach them as non-existent, they would be realized as such even if he had not said "dharmas of a Buddha." Because that essence of theirs does not exist, therefore the Tathāgata taught "dharmas of a Buddha" for the purpose of convention. Similarly, for all other things as well, this uncommon meaning itself should be known as being endowed with meaning. The dharmas of a Buddha should also be known here as the perfections, the articles of attraction, the applications of mindfulness, and so forth, the factors of awakening. By this, through the manner of completely abandoning the two extremes, it should be stated that for those bodhisattvas there is no deterioration of deteriorating due to the antidote. ^1-18

### Teaching the Stages ^1-6-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-19]]

What is the stage? The stage is here asserted as three aspects: the stage of conduct through aspiration, the stage of pure special resolve, and the stage endowed with the Tathāgata. In that regard, the stage of conduct through aspiration is taught by the sixteen supports, while through clear realization is the stage of pure special resolve. Through completely seeking the dharmas of a Buddha is the stage endowed with the Tathāgata. ^1-19

### Teaching the Reason for the Title ^1-7-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^1-20]]

What is the reason for the name? That which is the name Vajracchedikā should also be known as connected with two aspects of meaning: it is taught that its correct practice destroys views and afflictions. Because of their subtlety and essence, they become a vajra; moreover, they are subtle because of being difficult to know, and they are the essence because of being difficult to abandon. In that regard, this cuts them through hearing, thinking, and meditating on the prajñāpāramitā. This is also the vajra, and this is also the cutter, and it is the shape of complete cutting, thus it is the Vajracchedikā. A vajra is to be drawn with its two ends being vast and its middle being gathered; because such a prajñāpāramitā is gathered in the middle, the stage of pure special resolve is completely taught. Through the beginning and the end being vast, the stage of conduct through aspiration and the stage endowed with the Tathāgata are completely taught. In that regard, the five topics of meaning should be known as correctly depending upon each subsequent one; and those stages are the correct support; the connection of the body of the sūtra has been explained. ^1-20

## Explaining the Meaning of the Words ^2-0

### Teaching the Uninterrupted Lineage of the Buddhas ^2-1-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-1]]

Now the meaning of the words will be expressed. For what purpose did the Bhagavān, when teaching the Dharma, dwell in the deportment of an ascetic? It is for the purpose of completely teaching with the thought to show that "Only an ascetic is able to realize this Dharma." ^2-1

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-2]]

Regarding "benefited with the supreme benefit," what is the benefit to the bodhisattvas? And how is it supreme? The application of benefit and happiness is the benefit, and it should be known as supreme by six causes: time, distinction, vastness, stability, pervasion, and family. How is it by time? Because that seeing of the Dharma is supreme itself. That also is a benefit to the bodhisattvas; it possesses the seen dharma because of happiness itself. It possesses the correct beyond because of benefit itself. How is it by distinction? That is the worldly meditative absorption of the outsiders, whereas here, by the supramundane meditative absorption, it is eminently distinguished from the benefit to the noble śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. How is it by vastness? Because the benefit is unsurpassed itself. How is it by stability? Because it is absolute itself. How is it pervasion? Because it is the very rendering of benefit to the continuums of oneself and others. How is it the stage of the family? Because the rendering of benefit to impure bodhisattvas is the most principal itself. With the supreme, it completely entrusts. How is it with the supreme? By six causes: from extensiveness, observing reality individually, entering into complete knowledge, non-deterioration, compassion, and being worthy of respect. ^2-2

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-3]]

How is it from extensiveness? Because of completely entrusting to the spiritual friends. How is it from individually observing reality? Because the benefited bodhisattvas generate the reality of rendering benefit to others. How is it from entering into complete knowledge? Because it brings about the complete knowledge that 'these are the benefits rendered by you other bodhisattvas.' By these very three, it should be known as from non-deterioration, from possessing compassion, and from being worthy of respect. For what purpose was it asked by the Elder Subhūti? By six causes: for the purpose of cutting off doubt, for the purpose of causing to enter into special aspiration, for the purpose of causing to enter subsequently into the profound meaning, for the purpose of non-reversal, for the purpose of generating intense joy, and for the purpose of the sublime Dharma remaining for a long time; it is the prajñāpāramitā for the purpose of the continuum of the lineage not being interrupted. In that regard, how do these not interrupt the lineage of the Buddha? For the purpose of cutting off the doubts of the doubtful; for the purpose of causing bodhisattvas whose continuums are not completely ripened and who desire merit to enter into special aspiration for the prajñāpāramitā from obtaining much merit; for the purpose of causing those whose continuums are completely ripened to enter into the profound meaning; for the purpose of those who are completely transformed not reversing due to obtaining much merit upon sustaining them; for the purpose of correctly generating joy in the very merit of realizing that Dharma by themselves for those of pure special resolve who have been benefited; and for the sake of the teaching of the Great Vehicle remaining for a long time in future times. In brief, it is for the purpose of correctly teaching the doubtful, for the purpose of causing bodhisattvas who desire merit and are completely ripened to correctly take it up, for the purpose of praising those who are completely transformed, and for the purpose of making those of pure special resolve correctly rejoice. The bodhisattvas are called sattva and mahāsattva because they are great by seven aspects of greatness. What are the seven greatnesses? The greatness of Dharma, mind, aspiration, resolve, accumulation, time, and going everywhere; whichever is appropriate for the stages of a bodhisattva. ^2-3

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-4]]

Why is that? Because it is taught, "since one has correctly entered the vehicle of the bodhisattvas"; it was asked properly by the Elder Subhūti concerning what is eminently distinguished from the very three aspects of awakening. Having given approval, "however many sentient beings are included in the inclusion of sentient beings by me" teaches the general defining characteristic. "Or born from eggs" and so forth teaches the specific defining characteristic; moreover, that should be known from the categories stating the distinctions of birth and abode: "eggs, wombs, heat and moisture, and miraculous births" is the category of birth. "Possessing form or formless" is the category of abode. "Possessing perception or non-perceiving, or neither perceiving nor non-perceiving" is the category of apprehending objects. "However many are designated by the designation of the realm of sentient beings" definitively teaches the very designation as Buddha of the realm of sentient beings in the occasion of those very two aspects of defining characteristics. "All shall be passed completely beyond sorrow" pursues the purpose of not being powerless by any means. "From family" intends that it is done with regard to all families such as "born from eggs" and so forth as was explained, but it is not from number. ^2-4

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-5]]

Likewise, because it is done with regard to those born from eggs or born from heat and moisture, and those possessing perception, non-perceiving, and neither perceiving nor non-perceiving, all sentient beings should by all means be applied to complete passing beyond sorrow by three causes: for the purpose of those born in unfree states going to a time of leisure, for the purpose of those born in leisure who are not completely ripened being completely ripened, and for the purpose of those who are completely ripened being liberated. From what? Because it is taught, "into the realm of passing beyond sorrow without remainder." It is the very saying, "there is no passing completely beyond sorrow." The Bhagavat has also taught passing beyond sorrow by way of enumerations, for the purpose of completely abandoning that of the first concentration and so forth. That is, even though they are disciplined by the buddhas with the aspect of a reflected human, the ultimate should also be attained. As to from what, "into the realm of passing beyond sorrow with remainder" was not taught. That is a common result, because it is the result of one's own previous karma and the instructions of the Buddha. Although not singularly final, [it is said] "from the very pervasion of suffering of embodied beings"; passing beyond sorrow with remainder is because it is the very result of human effort, because it is the very common result, and because it is the very ultimate. Even from the very result that is not thoroughly final, it is explained as exactly what is called "without remainder." ^2-5

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-6]]

"Even though immeasurable sentient beings are thus passed completely beyond sorrow" completely shows as immeasurable individually the families such as "born from eggs" and so forth; it intends that "not any sentient being at all becomes passed beyond sorrow." What is the reason for that saying "there is not any at all" if it is said that "they are completely passed beyond sorrow by the bodhisattva himself"? "If a perception of a sentient being occurs in a bodhisattva, he should not be called a bodhisattva." What is the intention? If a perception of others as sentient beings occurs in a bodhisattva, due to perceiving a self, he should not be called a bodhisattva; what is the intention of what is said, "whoever has a perception of a sentient being, or a perception of a living being, or a perception of a person"? For whoever has the perception apprehending an afflicted sentient being, living being, and person occur, the perception of a self in that self also occurs, and the perception of a sentient being in a sentient being also occurs; in a bodhisattva, because of the very fact of having abandoned the view of a self, those do not occur. Therefore, the intention of this is that the bodhisattva who aspires to the very equality of self and others due to being equivalent in the defining characteristic of conditioned phenomena does not possess the view apprehending a sentient being, a living being, and a person. ^2-6

By saying, 'Whatever is the perception of a self, that itself is no perception,' it teaches that there is no apprehension as a dharma. ^2-63

'Why is that?' #### Endurance of Suffering ^2-3-13-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-68]]

In terms of being isolated from not bearing suffering, it was extensively spoken, 'Moreover, Subhūti, the Tathāgata's perfection of patience.' Regarding this too, if [one asks], 'By what sign should the patience just spoken of be known? What is the aspect of patience? What is the cause [and] antidote of patience?' That will be stated: The complete teaching, 'How is there patience? It is like that because of individually realizing the selflessness of dharmas,' is what was spoken as, 'Whatever is the Tathāgata's perfection of patience, that itself is no perfection.' If [one asks] by what sign patience is to be known: if, while harm is being done, there is the perception of a malicious mind from the basis of the perception of a self and so forth. Since there is no perception of this even as the perfection of patience, and there is also no perception of there being no perfection, its aspect is taught. ^2-68

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-69]]

It was extensively spoken, 'At the time when the king of Kaliṅga cut off my limbs and minor limbs, at that time I had no perception of a self.' If [one asks], 'With what kind of aspect did he bear it?' He bore intense suffering and he also bore continuous suffering; in order to completely teach what that is like, what was spoken is, 'At the time when the king of Kaliṅga cut off my limbs and minor limbs.' ^2-69

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-70]]

Saying, 'Moreover, I completely know that in past times, for five hundred lifetimes, I became the sage named Speaker of Patience,' means the cause of patience is also the three aspects of suffering: the suffering of saṃsāra, the suffering of beings acting wrongly, and the suffering of being separated from enjoyments. ^2-70

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-71]]

Regarding that, what was spoken is, 'Subhūti, therefore the bodhisattva mahāsattva should completely abandon all perceptions and generate the mind toward unsurpassed perfectly complete awakening.' This teaches the antidote to the cause of not bearing the suffering of saṃsāra. Generating the mind toward awakening also has three aspects: not desiring to generate it simply because of the perception of suffering; therefore it was extensively spoken, 'having completely abandoned all perceptions.' Here these are three aspects: the perception of suffering, all perceptions of wishing to abide in form and so forth, and not generating the mind toward awakening because the mind is completely wearied by these sufferings of saṃsāra. ^2-71

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-72]]

Saying, 'The mind should be generated without abiding in form,' is like before. ^2-72

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-73]]

Likewise, it was spoken, 'not abiding in dharmas, and also not abiding in no dharmas.' 'In dharmas' means not abiding even in the selflessness of dharmas; not abiding even in that itself, in order to negate conceptualizing the nature here as being literal, it was extensively spoken, 'because whatever is abiding, that itself is not abiding.' ^2-73

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-74]]

Moreover, what was spoken as, 'The bodhisattva should completely give generosity in order to benefit all sentient beings,' and what was extensively spoken as, 'Wherever whatever is the perception as a sentient being, that itself is also no perception'—this completely teaches the antidote to the cause of not bearing the suffering of sentient beings acting wrongly; it states that one completely gives for the sake of the benefit of all sentient beings themselves. If, regarding that itself, the action and the sentient being itself that adheres to the thought 'I am the agent' do not even exist, saying 'From what cause is there a perception as a sentient being, such that it generates weariness toward sentient beings acting wrongly?' completely teaches the selflessness of persons and the selflessness of dharmas as well. ^2-74

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-75]]

If [one asks], 'What is taught by what was extensively spoken, 'Subhūti, the Tathāgata speaks truly'?' It means, 'One should bear it also through genuine trust in the Tathāgata.' Regarding that, by virtue of the characteristic of conventional truth, he speaks truly. By virtue of the characteristics of affliction and purification that establish that, he speaks truthfully. Saying, 'This is to be afflicted and this is to be purified,' is the establishment of truth. ^2-75

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-76]]

By virtue of the characteristic of ultimate truth, he speaks suchness; by virtue of the characteristics of affliction and purification that establish that, it was spoken, 'speaks suchness' and 'speaks truly'. Here too, 'truly' is in order to completely abandon adhering to the literal nature; therefore it was spoken, 'In whatever dharma the Tathāgata has manifestly awakened to or taught, there is neither truth nor falsehood.' There is no truth because it does not exist with a literal nature. There is no falsehood because it exists with a non-literal nature. ^2-76

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-77]]

What was extensively spoken as, 'Subhūti, it is like this, for example: a person who has entered the darkness,' completely teaches the antidote to the cause of not bearing the suffering of incomplete enjoyments. Whoever gives generosity for the sake of enjoyments, that one gives generosity by falling into things; falling into that is for the sake of desiring happiness and being free from suffering. ^2-77

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-78]]

Feelings do not generate definite emergence; just as one who has entered the darkness thinks, 'How shall I go?'—therefore, rejoicing in the very happiness of desires, whatever generosity is given by not falling into things, that is viewed just as 'a person with eyes, when dawn has broken and the sun has risen, seeing various forms, [thinks] from where shall I go?'. When the night of ignorance has passed and the sun of pristine awareness has risen, that generates seeing the various aspects of knowables genuinely as they are. Otherwise, because there is definite emergence from feelings of suffering and the happiness of desires, therefore there is no rejoicing in the happiness of desires. ^2-78

#### Manifest Realization ^2-3-17-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-105]]

With respect to manifest realization, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, suppose, for example, a person were endowed with a human body and became a large body.' This clearly shows the obtainment of the pristine awareness of manifest realization and the absence of manifest pride. If [one asks], 'How is it through obtaining pristine awareness?' It is by taking up two kinds of pristine awareness: the pristine awareness that completely apprehends the lineage and the pristine awareness of equality. As for the pristine awareness through the obtainment of which one is born into the lineage of the Tathāgata, one becomes determined in the lineage for [becoming] a Buddha; that is the pristine awareness that completely apprehends the lineage, and by obtaining it one becomes endowed with a body. By this, wherever one takes manifest joy over many lifetimes, that one who has been born into the lineage becomes endowed with a body; therefore, one should be stated to be 'endowed with a body.' The pristine awareness of equality is also by taking up five kinds of equality: the equality of bad states, the equality of selflessness, the equality of striving to abandon, the equality of striving for a mind free of material things, and the equality of the manifest realization of all bodhisattvas. By hearing those, one becomes a large body, because of taking up the bodies of all sentient beings. That body of his is able to abide in what is neither his own nor others'. ^2-105

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-106]]

Therefore, it was extensively spoken, 'Whatever the Tathāgata has spoken of as a person endowed with a human body and a large body, the Tathāgata has spoken of that as not having a body; therefore...' Regarding that, by 'endowed with a body' and 'a large body', the ultimate mode is established here; thus, for the moment, it is from the obtainment of pristine awareness. ^2-106

One who is endowed with a view in that regard engages in genuine accomplishment; therefore, in order to abandon that, the ultimate is established: 'because they were spoken of by the Tathāgata as not being arrays, therefore they are called the arrays of the field'. ^2-110

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-111]]

Saying 'Subhūti, if any bodhisattva resolves this: "dharmas are selfless, dharmas are selfless"' was spoken with respect to two kinds of selflessness: the selflessness of the person and the selflessness of dharmas. Saying 'the Tathāgata states of him "bodhisattva, bodhisattva"' was spoken with respect to the two kinds of completely awakening to those very two kinds of selflessness. If [one asks], 'What is made clear by this?' It is that the apprehension of a person as in 'I will accomplish,' and the apprehension of dharmas as in 'the arrays of the field'—those do not exist for bodhisattvas. ^2-111

##### Excellence of Unsurpassable Vision and Pristine Awareness ^2-3-18-2-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-112]]

With respect to the perfection of unsurpassable seeing and pristine awareness, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think of this? Do you think the Tathāgata has the fleshly eye?' In order to make known that the Tathāgata possesses the preeminent completely pure seeing, he clearly shows that he does not possess only the eye of prajñā, but possesses five kinds of eyes. Otherwise, completely pure seeing would be sought by the eye of prajñā alone. Regarding that, when abbreviated, there are four kinds of eyes: that which apprehends form, that which apprehends the ultimate truth, that which apprehends the relative truth, and that which apprehends all objects of knowledge in all aspects. That which apprehends form is also of two kinds: the result of karma and the result of meditation. Therefore, there become five kinds. Because of possessing an extensive object, that which apprehends form is the first. By the power of knowing the ultimate, the knowledge of the relative engages unmistakably; therefore, that which apprehends the ultimate truth is prior. Regarding that, what is manifestly expressed as dharmas and persons is imputed as dharmas and persons. Knowing those is to be called the eye of dharma; this is the completely pure seeing of however much there is. ^2-112

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-113]]

Whatever was spoken as 'I fully know the stream of minds of various thoughts of those made as examples'—this is completely pure pristine awareness. Regarding that, the stream of mind is the minds of the three times. Regarding that, various thoughts should be known as being of two kinds: thoroughly afflicted and completely purified, or with desire and free from desire, and so forth. Alternatively, it is by the division of the past and so forth. ^2-113

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-114]]

In that way, both are established in the ultimate: it was extensively spoken 'what is called the stream of mind is without a stream' and 'the past mind is also unapprehendable'. Regarding that, the past mind is unapprehendable because it is utterly pacified. The future is because it has not arisen. The present is in the ultimate; seeing is established with respect to the entity of the object of knowledge of direct perception. Then, it is pristine awareness with respect to instructing sentient beings in accordance with their thoughts: whatever was spoken here as 'the stream of mind is without a stream'. If [one asks], 'Likewise, regarding completely pure seeing, why was it not spoken "the eye is without an eye"?' It is because pristine awareness and completely pure seeing have a single support, and from the establishment of the ultimate limit, it becomes established from the very beginning. ^2-114

##### Excellence of Mastery Over the Heap of Merit ^2-3-18-3-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-115]]

With respect to the perfection of mastery over the heap of merit, it was extensively spoken: 'by whoever this three-thousand great thousand world system'. ^2-115

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-116]]

Here also, the ultimate is established: it was extensively spoken, 'Moreover, Subhūti, if it were a heap of merit'. ^2-116

##### Excellence of Body ^2-3-18-4-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-117]]

With respect to the perfection of the body and the perfection of the body of minor marks, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think of this? Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the complete accomplishment of the form body?' ^2-117

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-118]]

With respect to the perfection of the body of marks, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think of this? Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of marks?' ^2-118

##### Excellence of Speech ^2-3-18-5-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-119]]

With respect to the perfection of speech, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think of this? Does the Tathāgata think this: "I have taught the Dharma"?' ^2-119

##### Excellence of Mind ^2-3-18-6-0

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-120]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, the establishment of mindfulness, it was extensively spoken: 'Bhagavān, in the future time, having heard such a teaching of the Dharma as this...' By this, the reason why the Bhagavān established his intention upon whichever sentient beings is completely and clearly taught: that they are not sentient beings is from the ultimate. That they are not without being sentient beings is from the conventional. What is other than this is from the teaching of the ultimate. Clearly teaching the meaning of the reasoning for the uncommon meanings is exactly as before for those very things. ^2-120

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-121]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, manifestly perfect awakening, it was extensively spoken: 'Do you think there is any Dharma at all by which the Tathāgata manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening?' Regarding that, having completely abandoned the fault of the view 'there is no such Dharma whatsoever', one completely seeks awakening and the path of awakening; moreover, that is taught by the two awakenings and the two definitive words. That is by grasping the word 'unsurpassed' and the word 'perfect'. 'a nu ra bi ta tra dharmo na bidya te' is grasping the word anuttara; it teaches the specific characteristic of awakening. In that awakening which has the characteristic of complete liberation, there is not even a particle of an arising dharma; for that very reason, it is unapprehendable. By that very thing, it should be known that 'rjes su yong', 'de la chos', 'yod pa', and 'na' mean 'does not exist'. ^2-121

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-122]]

By 'Moreover, Subhūti, samaḥ sa dharmaḥ', grasping the word 'perfectly complete', it teaches the general characteristic of persons from awakening. 'Regarding that, that Dharma is equal' is whatever Dharma by which one is designated as awakening and Buddha. ^2-122

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-123]]

By knowing 'regarding that, there is no inequality whatsoever', it teaches that for all Buddhas, in the ultimate, even their lifespan and so forth are without inequality. ^2-123

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-124]]

'That unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening is equal by being without a life force' - so it is said. ^2-124

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-125]]

Teaching awakening from the general characteristic of the dharmas of saṃsāra, it was spoken: 'by all virtuous dharmas one manifestly awakens'. Completely teaching the path of awakening is: 'those are also like that'; those are also established by the characteristic of the ultimate. ^2-125

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-126]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, the establishment of the great meaning, it was extensively spoken: 'however many Mount Merus there are in the three-thousand great thousand world system'. ^2-126

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-127]]

With respect to the establishment of the great meaning, the instructions, it was extensively spoken: 'if one thinks the Tathāgata thinks this: "I have liberated sentient beings"'. ^2-127

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-128]]

What is the intention behind saying, 'By that itself, the Tathāgata would have the grasping at a self'? Because the Tathāgata knows the knowable exactly as it is, therefore if he were to think of sentient beings, one should view that as 'by that itself, the Tathāgata would have the grasping at a self'. ^2-128

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-129]]

In order to abandon the manifest attachment thinking that, even if there is no self, for the time being there is the grasping at a self, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, as for what is called grasping at a self, that is without grasping'. ^2-129

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-130]]

Regarding 'even if childish ordinary beings grasped it as existing, it is ungraspable': 'Subhūti, as for what are called childish ordinary beings, they are without beings' was extensively spoken; those are just like that. ^2-130

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-131]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, the complete apprehension of the Dharmakāya, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think? Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of marks?'. Regarding that, by the first verse, how one does not see and by whom one does not see are clearly taught. If it is asked how one does not see, it is by the conventions of seeing and so forth. What is expressed as abandoning is meditation; it is expressed that one whose meditation is that is thus an abandoner, and that is also thinking and meditating. Regarding that, by thinking, one grasps it as not being that, and by meditating, it is wrong knowing; thus by having engaged through abandoning, one should know that the convention of wrong knowing is grasped. If it is asked by whom one does not see, it is by the beings who have engaged in conventions. ^2-131

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-132]]

By the former half of the second verse, what one does not see, and by the latter half, by what cause one does not see, are clearly taught. Regarding that, 'the Buddhas are to be seen as Dharmatā' is thinking of this Dharma as the meaning of the Tathāgata. ^2-132

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-133]]

If it is asked by what cause it is known, it was spoken: 'the guides are the Dharmakāya'. Because the Buddhas whose observing of suchness goes everywhere are the completely pure body, therefore they are to be seen as Dharmatā; this is to be seen as that. If it is asked moreover by what cause is it not to be seen, because Dharmatā, the characteristic of suchness, is not to be cognized literally, and because it is exactly to be individually reflexively known, by not being literal, through not seeing, one is not able to cognize; clearly teaching this meaning, it was spoken: 'since Dharmatā is not to be known, / by that one is not able to cognize'. Likewise here, that the support of the Dharmakāya is not to be seen as the Tathāgata is taught by 'not by the perfection of marks': the perfection of marks is not to be seen as the Tathāgata. ^2-133

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-134]]

In order to abandon the manifest attachment thinking that from the cause of the perfection of marks one found unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening, it was thus extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, what do you think? If you hold the thought that by the perfection of marks one manifestly perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening'; for those, it is like that. ^2-134

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-135]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, the non-abiding in saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, it was extensively spoken: 'Subhūti, moreover if you think "by those who have correctly entered the Bodhisattva vehicle"'. Regarding that, not designating the destruction of any dharma whatsoever is Dharmatā just as it abides. Being without annihilation is by individual realization; since the dharmas of saṃsāra have been mastered as being like reflections and emanations, one again thoroughly engages in the welfare of sentient beings. By this, for the time being, through refuting the suchness that goes exclusively to peace, the non-abiding in nirvāṇa itself is clearly taught. ^2-135

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-136]]

Then, in order to thoroughly abandon associating with complete affliction in saṃsāra while non-abiding in nirvāṇa, it was thus extensively spoken: 'Moreover, Subhūti, if a son of noble family...'. Regarding that, if it is asked what is the meaning of 'to the dharmas that are selfless and without arising', the mastery found by the Tathāgata over the emanation-like nature of the thorough engagement of whatever dharma, which is not the nature of the dharmas of saṃsāra and likewise which does not arise from karma and afflictions and which has become mastered, is called 'selfless and without arising'. If it is asked what makes this known: since even elsewhere if by completely grasping the merit just as it was explained one does not become completely afflicted in saṃsāra, what need to mention the Bodhisattvas' patience regarding the selfless and without arising? It is because by attaining patience for non-arising, much more merit will arise. ^2-136

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-137]]

'Moreover, Subhūti, a Bodhisattva should not completely grasp a heap of merit' clearly teaches the very non-abiding in saṃsāra; by not thoroughly abiding in saṃsāra, one completely grasps a heap of merit. ^2-137

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-138]]

Venerable Subhūti requested: 'If it is asked what is the intention of "one should not completely grasp a heap of merit", elsewhere the Bhagavat has spoken "one should completely grasp a heap of merit".' ^2-138

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-139]]

The Bhagavat stated: 'Although one completely grasps, one does not grasp wrongly; thus "one should completely grasp" teaches what is to be completely grasped by an enumeration. How one does not grasp wrongly is just as previously explained; it is exactly those.' ^2-139

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-140]]

With respect to the perfection of mind, the deportment of moving and abiding, it was extensively spoken: 'Moreover, Subhūti, whoever would speak thus: "the Tathāgata went or came"'. Regarding that, moving is walking and standing. The rest is deportment. ^2-140

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-141]]

With respect to moving and abiding, having obtained mastery over the meditation on the body of form and name, it was extensively spoken: 'Moreover, Subhūti, if any son of noble family or daughter of noble family... however many particles of earth there are in the great trichiliocosm world system'. Here also there are the method of making subtle and the method of making non-apparent; their meditation should be known as before. By 'do you think that collection of subtle particles is many?', here it refers to 'however many', which is the method of making subtle. ^2-141

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-142]]

'Bhagavat, if a collection existed, the Bhagavat would not have spoken of "a collection of subtle particles"' and so forth is the method of making non-apparent. If it is asked why it was explained thus: it is because if a collection of subtle particles existed ultimately, the Bhagavat would not have spoken of it as 'a non-collection'. ^2-142

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-143]]

Because the Bhagavat has spoken of that collection of subtle particles as a non-collection, it was explained thus. ^2-143

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-144]]

If it is asked why it is restated that whatever is a collection of subtle particles is unestablished in the nature of a collection, which becomes the idea of a collection even when the other is not explained: knowing that the Tathāgata has spoken of it as a non-realm is the very method of making non-apparent, and meditation on the body of name should be known as before. Here, the world of the three realms intends the world of sentient beings, and that is also completely distinguished by the collection of names. ^2-144

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-145]]

'Bhagavat, if a world system existed, it would be grasped as a whole' was spoken with respect to both whatever is a world system and whatever are subtle particles. Grasping as a whole is of two aspects: grasping as a whole as a single unit, and grasping as a whole by distinguishing. Regarding those, what exists in a world system would be grasped as a whole as a single unit as 'sentient beings in the aggregates'; what exists in subtle particles would be grasped as a whole by distinguishing, as it is grasped as a whole by grasping a collection of subtle particles. ^2-145

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-146]]

Here, 'grasping' is also posited ultimately by the noble Subhūti, and the Bhagavat establishes that very meaning: 'grasping as a whole is just a convention' and so forth extensively; if it is asked by what it is known, it is from convention. ^2-146

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-147]]

That grasping as a whole is also due to being inexpressible ultimately. Also, what is grasped by childish ordinary individuals is just according to convention, not ultimately. Having explained the meditation on the method of making non-apparent, the yoga and meditative absorption of making non-apparent are also established; and what is called 'non-conceptual' means not conceptualizing just as it is. ^2-147

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-148]]

Showing whoever, by what, and into what one does not conceptualize prior to that: 'Subhūti, if anyone would speak thus: "the Tathāgata has spoken of a view of self"' and so forth was spoken extensively; by this, how one does not conceptualize is clearly taught. ^2-148

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-149]]

If it is asked how: what is expressed as self by other non-Buddhists and what is spoken of as self by the Tathāgata are posited as the selflessness of persons. Speaking of that view of self as a non-view posits the selflessness of phenomena; if one applies it such that there is no grasping at a view regarding that view of self, one will attain meditative absorption in yoga; thus that non-conceptualization is the method for entering it. ^2-149

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-150]]

'Subhūti, thus one who has rightly entered the vehicle of the bodhisattvas', this completely teaches who does not conceptualize. 'All phenomena', this teaches regarding what one should not conceptualize. 'Should be known, should be viewed, should be resolved upon', this teaches that by which one should not conceptualize, namely, by knowing, seeing, and resolving, beginning from the higher mind and higher wisdom. Regarding those, what relies on serenity is wisdom. What relies on insight is exalted knowledge. Both seeings are here the samādhi of resolution; by the power of samādhi one intensely resolves upon the objective support of a reflected image. ^2-150

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-151]]

By 'one should resolve in such a way that one does not abide even in the perception of a phenomenon', whatever is conceptualization is completely taught; it is exactly those. 'The perception of a phenomenon' teaches the logical meaning of the uncommon meaning; this is the method of meditative absorption into yoga and should be known in all bases. Aspiration, prayer, and eradicating distraction are to be done to exactly that extent. Therefore, the method has been explained by those; it is exactly like that. ^2-151

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-152]]

Teaching the perfection of mind, with regard to being without affliction, it was spoken extensively: 'Moreover, Subhūti, by whatever bodhisattva great being...'. If it is asked what this teaches: Because this Dharma possesses great meaning, it is undoubtedly to be correctly completely illuminated. Although that is so, it is to be completely illuminated in such a way that one does not become afflicted at all. ^2-152

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-153]]

If it is asked how one correctly completely teaches: It is in such a way that one does not completely teach at all; therefore it is called 'correctly completely teaching'. If it is asked what the intention of this is: The Dharma whose nature is taught as inexpressible is not something to be taught; otherwise, it would be taught with affliction, because the meaning would be mistaken; teaching it in that way, there is no intense faith and seeking. Therefore, the Dharma is taught without affliction; that is exactly like that. ^2-153

![[bo-vasubandhu-saptartha-tika#^2-154]]

Teaching the perfection of mind, with regard to being without affliction in cyclic existence, there is the verse, 'A star, a visual aberration, a butter lamp...' The meaning is just as before and has already been explained. ^2-154
