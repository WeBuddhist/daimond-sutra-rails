## Mind and Mental Factors ^10-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-1]]

Subhūti, the past mind cannot be apprehended. ^10-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-2]]

The future mind cannot be apprehended. ^10-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-3]]

Because the present mind cannot be apprehended. ^10-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-4]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-5]]

If someone were to completely fill this three-thousandfold great thousandfold world realm with the seven precious substances and give it as a gift, would that son of noble family or daughter of noble family produce much merit on that basis? ^10-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-6]]

Subhūti said, "Blessed One, it is much." ^10-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-7]]

"Sugata, it is much." ^10-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-8]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, it is so; it is so." ^10-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-9]]

That son of noble family or daughter of noble family would produce a great heap of merit on that basis. ^10-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-10]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if a heap of merit were a heap of merit, the Tathāgata would not speak of 'a heap of merit, a heap of merit'. ^10-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-11]]

Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-12]]

Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfect accomplishment of the form body? ^10-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-13]]

Subhūti said, "Blessed One, it is not so." ^10-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-14]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfect accomplishment of the form body. ^10-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-15]]

Why is that? Blessed One, it is because what is called the 'perfect accomplishment of the form body' has been spoken of by the Tathāgata as no perfect accomplishment. ^10-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-16]]

Therefore it is called the 'perfect accomplishment of the form body'. ^10-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-17]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, what do you think?" ^10-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-18]]

Do you think the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of marks? ^10-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-19]]

Subhūti said, "Blessed One, it is not so." ^10-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-20]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of marks. ^10-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-21]]

Why is that? Because whatever perfection of marks has been spoken of by the Tathāgata, it has been spoken of by the Tathāgata as no perfection of marks. ^10-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-22]]

Therefore it is called the 'perfection of marks'. ^10-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-23]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, what do you think?" ^10-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-24]]

If you think the Tathāgata thinks, 'I have taught the Dharma,' Subhūti, do not view it like that. ^10-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-25]]

Because there is no Dharma whatsoever that has been taught by the Tathāgata. ^10-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-26]]

Subhūti, if anyone says that the Tathāgata has taught the Dharma, Subhūti, they would slander me by grasping at what is non-existent and false. ^10-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-27]]

Why is that? Subhūti, as for what is called 'teaching the Dharma', there is no Dharma whatsoever that can be apprehended as 'teaching the Dharma'. ^10-27

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-28]]

Then the venerable Subhūti said this to the Blessed One: ^10-28

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-29]]

"Blessed One, in the future, will there be any sentient beings who, having heard such a teaching of the Dharma, will come to truly believe it?" ^10-29

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-30]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, they are neither sentient beings, nor are they not sentient beings. ^10-30

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-31]]

Why is that? Subhūti, as for what are called 'sentient beings', it is because they have been spoken of by the Tathāgata as no sentient beings. ^10-31

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-32]]

Therefore they are called 'sentient beings'." ^10-32

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-33]]

"Subhūti, what do you think? ^10-33

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-34]]

Do you think there is any dharma whatsoever that the Tathāgata has fully awakened to as the unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening?" ^10-34

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-35]]

The venerable Subhūti said, "Blessed One, there is no dharma whatsoever that the Tathāgata has fully awakened to as the unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening." ^10-35

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-36]]

The Blessed One said, "Subhūti, so it is, so it is. ^10-36

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-37]]

In it, not even the slightest dharma is apprehended or exists; ^10-37

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-38]]

therefore it is called 'unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening.' ^10-38

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-39]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, that dharma is equal; ^10-39

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-40]]

because there is nothing whatsoever unequal or equal in it, therefore it is called 'unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening'." ^10-40

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-41]]

That unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening is equal in being without a self, without a sentient being, without a living soul, and without a person; ^10-41

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-42]]

it is fully awakened to by means of all wholesome dharmas. ^10-42

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-43]]

Subhūti, as for what are called 'wholesome dharmas', the Tathāgata has spoken of them as being exactly non-dharmas; ^10-43

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-44]]

therefore they are called 'wholesome dharmas'. ^10-44

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-45]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, compared to any son of noble family or daughter of noble family who, having collected heaps of the seven precious substances equal in amount to however many Sumerus, kings of mountains, there are in the three thousand grand thousand world systems, gives them as a gift, if someone were to take up even just a single four-line stanza from this perfection of wisdom and teach it to others, Subhūti, that former heap of merit does not approach even a hundredth part of this one, up to not even bearing comparison. ^10-45

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-46]]

Subhūti, what do you think of this? ^10-46

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-47]]

If you think the Tathāgata intends, 'I shall liberate sentient beings,' Subhūti, it should not be seen like that. ^10-47

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-48]]

Why is that? Because, Subhūti, there are no sentient beings whatsoever who are liberated by the Tathāgata. ^10-48

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-49]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to have liberated any sentient beings whatsoever, that itself would be an apprehension of a self by the Tathāgata, ^10-49

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-50]]

an apprehension of a sentient being, an apprehension of a living soul, and an apprehension of a person. ^10-50

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-51]]

Subhūti, as for what is called 'apprehension of a self', the Tathāgata has spoken of it as non-apprehension; yet it is apprehended by childish ordinary beings. ^10-51

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-52]]

Subhūti, as for what are called 'childish ordinary beings', the Tathāgata has spoken of them as being exactly non-beings; ^10-52

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-53]]

therefore they are called 'childish ordinary beings'. ^10-53

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-54]]

Subhūti, what do you think of this? ^10-54

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-55]]

Do you think that the Tathāgata is to be seen by the perfection of his marks? ^10-55

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-56]]

Subhūti said, 'Blessed One, it is not so.' ^10-56

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-57]]

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of his marks.' ^10-57

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-58]]

The Blessed One said, 'Subhūti, it is so. It is exactly so; ^10-58

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-59]]

the Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of his marks. ^10-59

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-60]]

Subhūti, if the Tathāgata were to be seen by the perfection of his marks, a wheel-turning king would also be a Tathāgata; ^10-60

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-61]]

therefore, the Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of his marks.' ^10-61

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-62]]

Then the venerable Subhūti spoke thus to the Blessed One: ^10-62

![[bo-vajracchedika#^10-63]]

'Blessed One, as I understand the meaning of what the Blessed One has spoken, the Tathāgata is not to be seen by the perfection of his marks.' ^10-63

## The Resultant Dharmakāya ^11-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-1]]

'Those who see me by form, those who know me by sound, have set out on a wrong path; ^11-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-2]]

those people do not see me. ^11-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-3]]

The Buddhas are to be seen as the nature of phenomena. ^11-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-4]]

The guides are the body of Dharma. Since the nature of phenomena is not an object of knowledge, it cannot be cognized.' ^11-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-5]]

'Subhūti, what do you think? ^11-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-6]]

If you think that the Tathāgata, the Arhat, became fully awakened to perfect and full awakening by the perfection of his marks, Subhūti, you should not see it like that. ^11-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-7]]

Subhūti, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, did not become fully awakened to perfect and full awakening by the perfection of his marks. ^11-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-8]]

Subhūti, if you think that those who have genuinely set out in the Bodhisattva vehicle have destroyed some phenomenon or designated it as annihilated, Subhūti, it should not be seen like that. ^11-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-9]]

Those who have genuinely set out in the Bodhisattva vehicle have not destroyed any phenomenon or designated it as annihilated. ^11-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-10]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if a son of good family or a daughter of good family were to fill as many world systems as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges with the seven precious things and give them as a gift, and if a Bodhisattva were to attain the patience that this Dharma-discourse is selfless and unproduced, he would produce from that basis a mass of merit vastly greater, incalculable and immeasurable. ^11-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-11]]

Moreover, Subhūti, a Bodhisattva should not fully acquire a mass of merit. ^11-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-12]]

Venerable Subhūti asked, 'Blessed One, should a Bodhisattva not fully acquire a mass of merit?' ^11-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-13]]

The Blessed One said, 'Subhūti, he does fully acquire it, but he does not acquire it erroneously; ^11-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-14]]

therefore it is called "fully acquiring".' ^11-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-15]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if anyone were to say that the Tathāgata goes, or comes, or stands, or sits, or lies down, he does not understand the meaning of what I have taught. ^11-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-16]]

Why is that? Subhūti, the one called "the Tathāgata" has not gone anywhere and has not come from anywhere. ^11-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^11-17]]

Therefore he is called the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened Buddha. ^11-17

## Subtle Particles ^12-0

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-1]]

Moreover, Subhūti, if any son of good family or daughter of good family were to grind as many particles of earth as there are in the trichiliocosm, the great thousandfold world system, into powder so that they become, for example, like a mass of fine atoms, what do you think, Subhūti, would that mass of fine atoms be large? ^12-1

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-2]]

Subhūti said, 'Blessed One, it is so; ^12-2

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-3]]

that mass of fine atoms would be large. ^12-3

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-4]]

Why is that? Blessed One, if a mass of fine atoms existed, the Blessed One would not have spoken of a "mass of fine atoms". ^12-4

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-5]]

Why is that? Because what the Blessed One has spoken of as a "mass of fine atoms", the Tathāgata has taught as no mass; ^12-5

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-6]]

therefore it is called a "mass of fine atoms". ^12-6

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-7]]

What the Tathāgata has spoken of as the "trichiliocosm, the great thousandfold world system", the Tathāgata has taught as no system; ^12-7

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-8]]

therefore it is called the "trichiliocosm, the great thousandfold world system".' ^12-8

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-9]]

Why is that? Blessed One, if a world system existed, that itself would be grasping at a solid mass. ^12-9

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-10]]

Because what the Tathāgata has spoken of as 'grasping at a solid mass', the Tathāgata has taught as no grasping; ^12-10

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-11]]

therefore it is called 'grasping at a solid mass'. ^12-11

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-12]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, 'grasping at a solid mass' is itself merely a convention; ^12-12

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-13]]

that dharma is inexpressible, yet it is grasped by ordinary childish beings. ^12-13

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-14]]

Subhūti, if anyone were to say: 'The Tathāgata has spoken of the view of a self, ^12-14

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-15]]

the Tathāgata has spoken of the view of a sentient being, the view of a living soul, and the view of a person'—if they said that, would they be speaking truthfully? ^12-15

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-16]]

Subhūti replied: Blessed One, they would not. ^12-16

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-17]]

Sugata, they would not. ^12-17

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-18]]

Why is that? Blessed One, what the Tathāgata has spoken of as the view of a self, the Tathāgata has spoken of as no view. ^12-18

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-19]]

Therefore it is called 'the view of a self'. ^12-19

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-20]]

The Blessed One said: Subhūti, thus should those who have truly entered the bodhisattva vehicle know, see, and be resolved upon all dharmas, ^12-20

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-21]]

they should know them in such a way that they do not abide in the perception of a dharma. ^12-21

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-22]]

Why is that? Subhūti, what is called 'perception of a dharma, perception of a dharma', the Tathāgata has spoken of as no perception. ^12-22

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-23]]

Therefore it is called 'perception of a dharma, perception of a dharma'. ^12-23

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-24]]

Furthermore, Subhūti, compared to a bodhisattva, a great being, who fills immeasurable and countless world systems with the seven precious things and gives them as a gift, if a son of noble family or daughter of noble family writes down, takes up, retains, reads, completely masters, or extensively and correctly explains to others even a four-line verse from this perfection of wisdom, they would produce infinitely and immeasurably more merit on that basis. ^12-24

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-25]]

How does one correctly explain it? Just as one does not correctly explain it, in that way one correctly explains it; ^12-25

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-26]]

Therefore it is called 'correctly explaining'. ^12-26

![[bo-vajracchedika#^12-27]]

Like a star, a visual aberration, a lamp, an illusion, a dewdrop, a bubble, a dream, lightning, and a cloud—view all conditioned things thus. ^12-27
